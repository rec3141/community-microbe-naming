# Canadian federal and Nunavut law, land-claim provisions and research-funding policy for environmental (microbial) DNA sampling and data with Inuit communities in Nunavut

Notes compiled 2026-10-29 for the Igluligaarjuk (Chesterfield Inlet) microbe-naming workshop dossier. This describes the rules; it is not legal advice. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from background knowledge or secondary sources and were not confirmed against primary text in this session. Primary texts of the Nunavut Agreement (nlca.tunngavik.com) returned HTTP 404 during this session, so article-level wording is partly unverified.

## 1. Nunavut Agreement (1993): relevant articles, land/water ownership, Inuit Owned Lands near Chesterfield Inlet

### Takeaway
The Nunavut Agreement does not have a "research" or "genetic resources" article. Sampling is governed indirectly: (a) land and water access rules (Arts. 19–21: Inuit Owned Lands (IOL) need consent from the Regional Inuit Association, here the Kivalliq Inuit Association); (b) the land-use planning and impact-screening system (Arts. 11–12: NPC conformity and NIRB screening, which NRI says is required for all new land- and water-based research); (c) wildlife management (Art. 5, NWMB). Marine waters and most large lake and seabed areas are generally Crown, not IOL. Whether microbes count as "wildlife" under the Agreement is not settled.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 21 (entry and access to IOL), general rule.** "Except where otherwise provided in the Agreement persons other than Inuit may not enter, cross or remain on Inuit Owned Lands without the consent of the DIO" (Designated Inuit Organization). For almost all access matters, the DIO is the Regional Inuit Association that holds title. — [NTI, IOL and Minerals presentation, 2009](https://www.tunngavik.com/documents/publications/administration/IOL%20and%20Minerals%2025Nov09.pdf); [Nunavut Agreement Art. 21 (nlca.tunngavik.com, search snippet; page 404 on fetch)](https://nlca.tunngavik.com/?p=2083)
- **Art. 21.5, government access.** Agents, employees and contractors of Government have a right to enter, cross and remain on IOL and the water on it. However, "Access to Inuit Owned Lands by Government personnel for the purposes of wildlife management and wildlife research shall be subject to the approval of the NWMB subsequent to consultation with the appropriate RWO" (Regional Wildlife Organization). — [Nunavut Agreement Art. 21 (search snippet)](https://nlca.tunngavik.com/?p=2083). University researchers are not "Government" and fall under the general consent rule (inference).
- **Kivalliq instrument.** A Government of Nunavut research-permitting guide lists the Kivalliq Inuit Association (KivIA) as the authority for "entry to and activities on Inuit owned lands in the Kivalliq Region". Its instrument is described as a *Certificate of Exemption*, whereas Kitikmeot and Qikiqtani issue "Access to Inuit Owned Lands Permits". — [GN research guide (Guide_English.pdf)](https://prod.gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/forms/2022-01/Guide_English.pdf) (search-snippet level; **[UNVERIFIED]** whether KivIA's current instrument is still called this; check [kivalliqinuit.ca](https://kivalliqinuit.ca) Lands department).
- NRI's 2025 guide lists "Permission from a Regional Inuit Association (RIA) to access or use Inuit Owned Lands for any purposes" as a separate authorization. — [NRI, *Obtaining a Research License under Nunavut's Scientists Act: A Guide for Applicants*, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Art. 19 (title to IOL).** There are two kinds of IOL title: fee simple including mines and minerals (subsurface IOL, held by NTI), and fee simple excluding mines and minerals (surface IOL, held by the RIA). — [NTI IOL presentation](https://www.tunngavik.com/documents/publications/administration/IOL%20and%20Minerals%2025Nov09.pdf). **[UNVERIFIED]** The marine areas and seabed of Hudson Bay off Chesterfield Inlet are not IOL; they are federal Crown (seabed) with territorial waters. Beds of large lakes are generally Crown unless a parcel's description includes them. The specific IOL parcels around Chesterfield Inlet, and whether the 2025 lake or shore sampling sites fall on IOL, municipal or Commissioner's land, have to be checked on NTI/KivIA IOL maps.
- **Who controls the water column.** Nunavut's Scientists Act applies "within the Territory of Nunavut, including Territorial waters (seaward up to 12 nautical miles)". — [NRI guide May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf). Water use is regulated by the Nunavut Water Board (Art. 13, implemented via the *Nunavut Waters and Nunavut Surface Rights Tribunal Act*, S.C. 2002, c. 10). — [Justice Laws, S.C. 2002 c.10](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/annualstatutes/2002_10/FullText.html). Inuit water rights on IOL are in Art. 20 **[UNVERIFIED article content]**.
- **Art. 5 (wildlife, NWMB).** The NWMB describes "wildlife" under the Agreement as land and marine mammals, birds, fish and plants in Nunavut. — [NWMB FAQ](https://www.nwmb.com/en/about-nwmb/faqs). **[UNVERIFIED]** The Art. 1 definition reads approximately "all terrestrial, aquatic, avian and amphibian flora and fauna ferae naturae and all parts and products thereof". This could not be confirmed against the primary text.
- **Arts. 11–12 (NPC/NIRB).** NRI says NPC review "is required for ALL new land- and water-based research in Nunavut", and NIRB screening "is required for ALL new land- and water-based research in Nunavut that is subject to licensing under the Scientists Act". "The NPC is the first point of entry ... and has the authority to exempt low-impact proposals from NIRB screening." NRI cannot issue a licence until NPC and NIRB are complete. — [NRI guide May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf). The statutory basis is the *Nunavut Planning and Project Assessment Act* (NuPPAA, S.C. 2013, c. 14, s. 2) **[UNVERIFIED citation details]**. Chesterfield Inlet falls under the Keewatin Regional Land Use Plan (2000) **[UNVERIFIED that it remains in force pending the Nunavut Land Use Plan; the draft NLUP was still under approval as of 2025–26 — check nunavut.ca]**.
- **Art. 32 (social and cultural policy)** requires Government to give Inuit an opportunity to participate in developing social and cultural policies and programs **[UNVERIFIED wording]**. **Art. 33 (archaeology)** covers archaeological sites and specimens and is administered with the Inuit Heritage Trust. — see [Nunavut Archaeological and Palaeontological Sites Regulations SOR/2001-220](https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-220/page-1.html). It applies only if sampling disturbs archaeological sites. Microbial water and sediment sampling normally does not.
- **Genetic resources.** A legal analysis says the Scientists Act only requires researchers to obtain an "access licence" and that Nunavut's regime for genetic resources is "rather inchoate". The Agreement has no ABS clause. — [Bannister/Cambridge, "Making room for the Nagoya Protocol in Nunavut" (Cambridge UP book chapter)](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E82D412D0E644936444E0B3FC25A2352/9781108470766c5_80-97.pdf/making_room_for_the_nagoya_protocol_in_nunavut.pdf) (author attribution **[UNVERIFIED]**).

### Inferences
- If any 2025 freshwater site was on IOL, KivIA consent was legally required before entry. If sites were on municipal land or Crown marine waters, no KivIA consent was needed for access, though KivIA is normally consulted by NRI anyway.
- Microbes are not clearly "wildlife" under Art. 5 ("ferae naturae" suggests wild animals and plants). Microbial sampling is therefore probably outside NWMB and Wildlife Act permitting and inside the Scientists Act. NRI licenses "hydrology, limnology, water quality" and "oceanography" as physical/natural sciences.
- None of these instruments assigns ownership of genetic information in microbes. Ownership of physical samples follows general property law and permit conditions (see Section 6).

### Gaps
- Exact Art. 1 wording of "wildlife", "marine areas" and IOL boundary rules (Art. 19 water bodies); primary site 404.
- IOL parcel map for Chesterfield Inlet and the current KivIA instrument name and process.
- Whether the 2025 project actually went through NPC/NIRB (project-specific; check the NPC public registry and NIRB registry by project name).

## 2. Scientists Act / NRI scientific research licence and other permits

### Takeaway
A Nunavut Scientific Research Licence from NRI is required both for the 2025 microbial sampling (physical/natural science: "collection of new research information on ... air, land, or water", including "collect[ing] specimens") and very likely for the workshop if it generates research outputs (social science or Inuit knowledge component). Community engagement visits and visits to share results are exempt. Licensing involves 45 business days of community consultation, translated (Inuktitut) summaries and consent forms, REB approval for human-participant components, prior NPC/NIRB clearance for field sampling, annual summary reports by December 31, and copies of publications to NRI.

### Cited Findings (all from [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf) unless noted)
- **Statutory rule (Scientists Act, R.S.N.W.T. 1988, c. S-4, s. 2, as duplicated for Nunavut):** "No person shall carry on scientific research in or based on Nunavut, or collect specimens in Nunavut for use in scientific research, unless ... he or she is the holder of a licence issued under this Act" (archaeology permits excepted).
- **Scope.** The Act applies to "health, social, and physical/natural science disciplines (i.e., projects that involve the collection of new research information on people, air, land, or water)". It "does not apply to research on wildlife as defined under the Nunavut Agreement, or to the collection of specimens of wildlife as defined in the Wildlife Act", nor to archaeology. Physical/natural topics listed include "hydrology, limnology, water quality" and "oceanography, sea ice".
- **Secondary and remote research.** A licence is also required for "secondary research involving the use of unpublished information already collected for a purpose other than the current research purpose" and for research "based on Nunavut" conducted remotely. The defining feature is intent to produce "authoritative scientific outputs (academic theses, peer-reviewed journal articles ...)".
- **Exemptions relevant to the workshop:** "consultations, engagement and outreach with community members to establish research partnerships or collaborations prior to the actual design of the research" and "visits to communities to disseminate research results". However: "Licensing is also usually required if projects have ancillary research objectives and/or involve Academic partners who intend to use the information gathered to produce academic, peer-reviewed research outputs." Researchers may travel without a licence "for the purpose of meeting informally ... conducting consultations, workshops, or other engagement activities aimed at co-designing your research." Final authority rests with the Science Advisor and Minister. Contact the Manager of Research Liaison at NRI (contact details on nri.nu.ca).
- **Process.** No fee. The application cannot enter consultation until it is complete with a "translated project summary, institutional ethics approval, and translated consent forms". Consultation runs a minimum of 45 business days with agencies such as Municipal Councils (Hamlet), HTOs (here the Aqigiq HTO **[UNVERIFIED name]**), District Education Authorities, RIAs (KivIA), NTI, IPGs and GN departments. Agencies "may recommend approval or rejection". Licences can be issued early if local authorities or community collaborators provide support letters. Social science and health applicants must show REB approval before issuance. The Science Advisor has up to one year to issue.
- **Multi-year.** Licences can cover up to 3 calendar years, renewed annually. Renew by December 31 for May–September field seasons, otherwise 120 days before fieldwork.
- **Reporting.** An Annual Summary Report is due within 6 months of expiry or at renewal (by December 31 for the next field season), even if no work was done. Researchers must "send NRI copies of any final publications or reports" and are "expected to share your research results directly with Nunavut agencies and community members ... develop a communication plan", plan return visits, and involve community members "in reviewing, interpreting, and validating your findings". Licences can be revoked for failure to report. The translated-summary requirement implies Inuktitut (Inuktut) plain-language summaries. **[UNVERIFIED]** that the annual summary must itself be translated; the FAQ refers to "a translated annual summary report" for cancelled projects.
- **Video.** "Do not collect video footage of wildlife or people without first notifying the Nunavut Film Development Corporation" (relevant if the workshop is filmed).
- **Other authorizations (NRI list):** Wildlife Research Licence (GN Dept. of Environment, *Wildlife Act*: terrestrial wildlife incl. plants, insects); DFO Licence to Collect Fish for Scientific Purposes (*Fisheries Act*: "marine/freshwater mammals, fish, invertebrates and fish habitat (including aquatic plants)"); SARA permits; CWS migratory-bird and National Wildlife Area permits; NPC review ("required for ALL new land- and water-based research"); NIRB screening; CIRNAC land-use permit (Crown land, >100 person-days or fuel caches); RIA permission for IOL; municipal development permit (permanent infrastructure); and "Authorization issued by the Nunavut Water Board to collect water samples or use potable water for a research camp."
- **Multi-disciplinary projects.** These need multiple licences. NRI's example: benthic invertebrates plus Inuit knowledge requires a DFO licence and an NRI licence (for the IQ component), plus NPC.
- NRI's guide asks applicants to read ITK's National Inuit Strategy on Research (2018) and TCPS 2 before applying.

### Inferences
- **2025 sampling.** Water, sediment and microbial DNA collection matches "limnology, water quality, oceanography" and "collect specimens", so an NRI licence plus NPC/NIRB (possibly exempted from NIRB by NPC as low-impact) and possibly NWB authorization were required. Bulk water and sediment sampling for microbes likely does not need DFO unless invertebrates or fish habitat are sampled. Wildlife Act permits likely do not apply (microbes are not listed).
- **Ongoing analysis** of samples (genome building, publication) continues "research based on Nunavut", so annual reports and publication copies continue to be owed while the licence is active.
- **Workshop.** If naming sessions, recorded Inuktitut terms, IQ about places and organisms, or participant feedback will feed into publications (e.g., SeqCode protologues, papers on community naming), this is a social science / Inuit knowledge component. It needs an NRI licence amendment or new licence (with REB approval and translated consent forms). If the workshop is purely sharing results and co-designing names that the community owns, it may fall under the "dissemination" or "engagement" exemption. Confirm with NRI in writing.
- The names published in the SeqCode registry are a public scholarly output, which pushes toward licensing.

### Gaps
- Whether the Scientists Act has been replaced or amended in 2023–2026 (it is an inherited NWT statute; no amendment was found).
- Current NWB practice: whether small-volume scientific water sampling actually triggers an NWB licence or is handled via NPC/NIRB only (the NRI guide says "authorization").

## 3. Inuit Language Protection Act and Official Languages Act (Nunavut)

### Takeaway
Nunavut's *Inuit Language Protection Act* (S.Nu. 2008, c. 17) and *Official Languages Act* (S.Nu. 2008, c. 10) make Inuktut an official language and require organizations offering public-facing services and communications in Nunavut to provide them in Inuktut. They do not regulate scientific naming. For a workshop, they mainly support (and arguably require for public-facing materials) Inuktut versions of handouts, signs and notices, and Inuktut terminology standardization is the remit of the Inuit Uqausinginnik Taiguusiliuqtiit.

### Cited Findings
- The ILPA requires every organization in Nunavut (businesses, NGOs, municipal, territorial and federal bodies) to provide public signs, posters, commercial advertising and reception/customer services in Inuktut, at least as prominently as other languages, with an "active offer". Private-sector provisions took effect July 9, 2017. Enforcement is by the Languages Commissioner, complaint-driven. — [Nunatsiaq News, 2017](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674new_inuktut_language_requirements_come_into_force_for_nunavut_organiza/); [GN "new language requirements"](https://prod.gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2021-11/new_language_requirements_eng_small_size_0.pdf); [Languages Commissioner, Inuktut Language Plan Guidelines](https://langcom.nu.ca/sites/langcom.nu.ca/files/InuitLanguagePlanGuidelinesEN.pdf)
- The Office of the Language Authority (Inuit Uqausinginnik Taiguusiliuqtiit, IUT) can review terminology and orthography. — [Languages Commissioner guidelines](https://langcom.nu.ca/sites/langcom.nu.ca/files/InuitLanguagePlanGuidelinesEN.pdf)
- NRI requires translated project summaries and consent forms before review. — [NRI guide May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** The ILPA also includes Inuit-language rights in education and in territorial government work, and a s. 1 purpose of protecting the language. The Kivalliq dialect (Kivallirmiutut / Aivilingmiutut) is relevant for Igluligaarjuk.

### Inferences
- A university running a public workshop in Chesterfield Inlet is probably an "organization" offering public communications. Inuktut versions of posters, handouts and slides are both legally prudent and expected by NRI and TCPS norms.
- SeqCode names must be Latinized and follow Latin grammar. Inuktitut roots will be transliterated from syllabics. Consulting IUT or local elders on spelling (Roman orthography of the ICI standard, plus syllabics on handouts) aligns with the Act's language-protection purpose. The Act does not grant or restrict rights over use of Inuktut words.

### Gaps
- No source found on any legal rule restricting use of Inuktut words in scientific names. None is believed to exist.
- Confirmation of the Act's current consolidated title (some sources refer to an "Inuktut Protection Act" rename; not confirmed).

## 4. UNDRIP Act (2021), Action Plan (2023), Inuit Nunangat Policy (2022), Inuit research and data strategies

### Takeaway
The *United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act* (S.C. 2021, c. 14) and its 2023–2028 Action Plan bind the federal government, not researchers directly. But they frame funder and federal-lab expectations: Inuit self-determination in research, protection of traditional knowledge and cultural expressions (including IP, a Shared Priorities measure, numbered "101" in a secondary source), and Inuit data sovereignty. Inuit organizations' own frameworks (ITK's National Inuit Strategy on Research 2018, the National Inuit Data Strategy and NTI's Nunavut Data Strategy) set the expectations researchers are measured against.

### Cited Findings
- **UNDA Action Plan.** Released June 21, 2023. It runs 2023–2028 with 181 measures assigned to departments; AFN reports implementation as "slow-moving". — [AFN UNDA Action Plan Quarterly Report](https://afn.bynder.com/m/1c5ad1ba762f19ca/original/UNDA-Action-Plan-Quarterly-Report.pdf); [IPIC summary, 2023](https://ipic.ca/news/federal-government-issues-action-plan-for-implementation-of-the-united-nations-declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples-act-2023-06-22)
- **Measure on TK and IP.** Shared Priorities includes a commitment to work with First Nations, Inuit and Métis on protections for cultural heritage, traditional knowledge and traditional cultural expressions, "including intellectual property", "in consultation and cooperation ... to ensure appropriate measures are in place". The search snippet attributes this to "item 101" (likely measure 1.101 **[UNVERIFIED numbering]**). — [IPIC summary](https://ipic.ca/news/federal-government-issues-action-plan-for-implementation-of-the-united-nations-declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples-act-2023-06-22); [UNDA draft action plan (Justice Canada)](https://justice.gc.ca/eng/declaration/ap-pa/ah/pdf/UNDA_Draft_Action_Plan.pdf). UNDRIP Art. 31 (right to maintain, control, protect and develop heritage, TK, "genetic resources, seeds, medicines, knowledge of the properties of fauna and flora") is the underlying norm **[from UNDRIP text, background knowledge]**.
- Annual progress reports exist (2022, 2023 and 2024 editions on publications.gc.ca). — [J1-31-2024](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2024/jus/J1-31-2024-eng.pdf)
- **Inuit-specific data measures.** An Inuit organization document calls for implementing the Inuit Data Strategy "to promote Inuit data sovereignty", along with Inuit research capacity and leadership in research ethics. — [Pauktuutit Brief 04: Research and Data (April 2026)](https://pauktuutit.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/PAUKTUUTIT-Brief-04-Research-and-Data-EN-FINAL_web.pdf) (search-snippet level).
- **National Inuit Strategy on Research (ITK, March 2018).** It has five priority areas **[from background knowledge: (1) advance Inuit governance in research; (2) enhance ethical conduct of research; (3) align funding with Inuit research priorities; (4) ensure Inuit access, ownership and control over data and information; (5) build capacity in Inuit Nunangat research]**. It is a guidance document with "no teeth or enforcement mechanisms". The Implementation Plan was published 2018 and updated 2020. — [Nunatsiaq News, "five things you need to know"](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674the_national_inuit_research_strategy_five_things_you_need_to_know/); [ITK NISR PDF](https://www.itk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/ITK_NISR-Report_English_low_res.pdf); [ITK NISR page](https://www.itk.ca/national-inuit-strategy-on-research/)
- **NTI and data sovereignty.** NTI leads Inuit research and data governance policy in Nunavut. It is developing a Nunavut Data Strategy and supports ITK's National Inuit Data Strategy. Principles: Inuit control research so it benefits Inuit; Inuit access to data from their communities; data handled per Inuit values; data shared "only for purposes and with parties that Inuit approve". — [Arctic Focus, "Upholding Inuit data sovereignty in Nunavut"](https://www.arcticfocus.org/stories/upholding-inuit-data-sovereignty-nunavut/) (undated; secondary)
- ICC has called for Inuit data sovereignty at the UN Expert Mechanism (EMRIP). — [Arctic Today](https://www.arctictoday.com/🇨🇦-icc-calls-for-inuit-data-sovereignty-and-protection-of-traditional-economies-at-un-expert-mechanism). ICC also publishes the *Circumpolar Inuit Protocols for Equitable and Ethical Engagement* (2022). — [ICC](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/project/circumpolar-inuit-protocols-for-equitable-and-ethical-engagement/)
- **Inuit Nunangat Policy.** Endorsed by the Inuit-Crown Partnership Committee in April 2022. **[UNVERIFIED — not fetched]** It commits federal departments to apply the policy when designing programs affecting Inuit Nunangat, including research programs, and to support Inuit self-determination in research. See [rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca Inuit Nunangat Policy](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1650556354784/1650556491509) (URL from background knowledge).

### Inferences
- For this project, these instruments are "soft law" setting the benchmark: Inuit governance of the research agenda, Inuit access to and control over data (including sequence data and genomes derived from their lands and waters), benefit to community, and capacity building.
- A workshop where community members name organisms fits NISR priority areas on governance and capacity. The data-management side (who decides on public release of genomes, the community's access to results) is where NISR priority 4 and the NTI principles bite.

- Exact Action Plan measure numbers for Inuit-specific research/data commitments and the TK/IP measure (the final plan PDF was not fetched).
- Status of the National Inuit Data Strategy and NTI's Nunavut Data Strategy releases as of October 2026 (not confirmed released).
- Text of the Inuit Nunangat Policy research provisions.

## 5. TCPS 2 (2022) Chapter 9

### Takeaway
TCPS 2 applies to research involving humans, so the workshop (participants, Inuit knowledge, language) is in scope, while pure environmental sampling is outside REB review. But Chapter 9 says community engagement is still expected for research on Inuit lands, and community customs may require permissions and reporting. Key articles: 9.1 (engagement when research is on Inuit lands or affects community welfare), 9.2 (nature of engagement decided jointly), 9.8 (respect community customs and codes of practice), 9.13 (mutual benefit), 9.17 (community participation in interpretation and dissemination), 9.18 (IP to be discussed and specified in a research agreement), 9.19 (human biological materials, not microbes), 9.20 (secondary use of data identifiable to an Indigenous community needs REB review).

### Cited Findings (all from [TCPS 2 (2022), Chapter 9](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter9-chapitre9.html))
- **Art. 9.1.** Where research "is likely to affect the welfare of an Indigenous community", researchers "shall seek engagement with the relevant community". Listed triggers include "research conducted on First Nations, Inuit or Métis lands". Researchers should learn the "formal rules or oral customs that may apply".
- **Art. 9.2.** The nature and extent of engagement "shall be determined jointly by the researcher and the relevant community". **Example (natural sciences on Indigenous lands):** research involving "the collection and analysis of tissue samples from animals or plants" is outside the Policy's scope and does not require REB review, but community customs may still require permissions and reporting.
- **Key concepts.** "Ethical obligations often extend to respectful relations with plant, animal, and marine life."
- **Art. 9.8.** Researchers "have an obligation to become informed about, and to respect, the relevant customs" and codes of research practice.
- **Art. 9.13.** "The research should benefit the participating community."
- **Art. 9.17.** Researchers "should afford community representatives engaged in collaborative research an opportunity to participate" in interpretation and review of findings before publication.
- **Art. 9.18.** IP rights "should be discussed by researchers, communities, and institutions", and assigned or licensed terms "should be specified in a research agreement".
- **Art. 9.19.** Human biological materials: rights and proprietary interests to be specified in a research agreement. This covers only human material.
- **Art. 9.20.** "Secondary use of data and human biological material identifiable as originating from an Indigenous community" is subject to REB review.
- NRI points applicants to TCPS 2. The NRI guide still cites the 2018 edition; the current edition is TCPS 2 (2022). — [NRI guide May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Environmental sampling and metagenomes.** Outside REB jurisdiction, but Art. 9.1/9.2 norms (engagement, permissions, reporting) apply as ethics guidance. NRI's licensing review operationalizes this in Nunavut.
- **Workshop.** Involves human participants contributing knowledge and language, so REB review is needed if it is "research" (data or outputs to be published). That means informed consent (translated), consent options for attribution versus anonymity of name contributors, a community research agreement covering IP in names and Inuit knowledge (Art. 9.18), and community review of interpretations before publication (9.17). Recording and using Inuit knowledge or names later for other purposes may be "secondary use" (9.20).
- Human DNA "bycatch" in environmental metagenomes is a human-biological-data edge case (Art. 9.19/9.20 and Chapter 12/13). Removing human reads before deposit is a prudent practice **[inference; no Nunavut-specific rule found]**.

### Gaps
- Whether any TCPS 2 revision after 2022 (e.g., a 2025/2026 edition) changed Chapter 9. None was found; check ethics.gc.ca.

## 6. Tri-Agency Research Data Management Policy (2021), Indigenous research guidance, rollout status

### Takeaway
The RDM Policy (2021) requires institutional RDM strategies (done by March 1, 2023), DMPs for specified funding opportunities (phased from spring 2022 and still expanding without a fixed timeline), and a data-deposit requirement that has not yet been fully phased in (no dates as of the page's December 19, 2025 update). For research "by and with" First Nations, Inuit and Métis, DMPs "must be co-developed" with communities, respect Indigenous data sovereignty, allow renegotiation, and may justify exceptions to data deposit.

### Cited Findings (all from [Tri-Agency RDM Policy, science.gc.ca, page modified 2025-12-19](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/interagency-research-funding/policies-and-guidelines/research-data-management/tri-agency-research-data-management-policy))
- **Preamble.** Data from research by and with First Nations, Métis and Inuit communities must be managed per principles developed and approved by those communities, based on free, prior and informed consent. Requires "a distinctions-based approach". OCAP® is named as one First Nations model. CARE is not named on the page.
- **Section 3.2 (DMPs).** For Indigenous research, DMPs "must be co-developed with these communities, collectives and organizations", following their RDM principles or formats, recognizing Indigenous data sovereignty and allowing renegotiation. DMPs cover collection and preservation methods, sharing and deposit plans, and a data manager and succession plan.
- **Section 3.3 (deposit).** Recipients must deposit data, metadata and code supporting conclusions in publications and preprints. Communities guide collection, use and preservation, and may repatriate data, which "could result in exceptions to the data deposit requirement". Data *sharing* is not mandatory.
- **Rollout.** Initial DMP-requiring opportunities named spring 2022. Institutional strategies due March 1, 2023. Further DMP expansion and the deposit phase-in have no dates as of the December 2025 update.
- **[UNVERIFIED — background knowledge]** Tri-agency *Setting New Directions to Support Indigenous Research and Research Training in Canada 2019–2022* (strategic plan) committed to Indigenous self-determination in research, including Indigenous data governance. NSERC has piloted DMP requirements in specific programs (e.g., some Alliance and Discovery Horizons calls). Check NSERC's current DMP page.

### Inferences
- If the 2025 work is NSERC-funded under a DMP-requiring program, the DMP should have been co-developed with Inuit partners (e.g., Hamlet, HTO, KivIA, or NTI). Even otherwise, the policy's Indigenous provisions express agency expectations.
- **Practical tension.** SeqCode registration and journal norms expect public deposit of genomes (INSDC). Under the RDM policy, Inuit-community direction can justify exceptions or conditions on deposit, so a co-developed DMP should record community decisions on public release, metadata (e.g., geographic precision, Inuktitut place names), Local Contexts Biocultural/TK Labels, and repatriation of data copies to a community-accessible store.

## 7. Access and benefit-sharing (ABS), traditional knowledge and IP, ownership of samples, data, genomes and names

### Takeaway
Canada is not a party to the Nagoya Protocol and has no comprehensive ABS system. No federal or Nunavut law grants Inuit statutory ownership of microbial genetic resources or sequence data. Ownership and use are therefore governed by contract (research agreements, licence conditions, KivIA land-access terms), institutional policy and ethics frameworks. Canadian IP law gives limited protection to traditional knowledge. Scientific names under SeqCode are not owned. Individual Inuktitut words are not copyrightable, though recorded oral texts and handouts can be.

### Cited Findings
- **Nagoya Protocol.** "Canada is not a Party to the Nagoya Protocol" and could accede at any time. There is "currently no single, comprehensive access and benefit-sharing (ABS) system in place in Canada". An interdepartmental committee has studied ABS and consulted provinces, territories and Indigenous communities. — [ECCC, Nagoya Protocol page (dated 2022-09-02)](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/corporate/international-affairs/partnerships-organizations/nagoya-protocol-access-genetic-resources.html). Commentators describe Canada's ABS process as having "ground to a halt" and failing to incorporate Indigenous peoples. — [ABS Canada initiative chapter, Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3C8B5FEA39E4B3CCA3FF9595917040A8/9781108470766c1_3-19.pdf/abs_canada_initiative.pdf); [ABS Canada, "Leader turned laggard"](https://abs-canada.org/leader-turned-laggard-canada-the-nagoya-protocol-and-the-road-ahead)
- **Digital sequence information.** **[UNVERIFIED — background knowledge]** At CBD COP16 (Cali, November 2024) parties adopted a multilateral mechanism for DSI benefit-sharing, including the "Cali Fund" with voluntary contributions by large users of DSI and an expectation that a share goes to Indigenous peoples and local communities. Canada is a CBD party, so the Cali decision applies politically but is not domestically binding on university researchers. ECCC's page does not mention DSI.
- Nunavut's Scientists Act provides only an access-type licence with no benefit-sharing terms. — [Cambridge chapter, "Making room for the Nagoya Protocol in Nunavut"](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E82D412D0E644936444E0B3FC25A2352/9781108470766c5_80-97.pdf/making_room_for_the_nagoya_protocol_in_nunavut.pdf)
- **TK and IP.** The UNDA Action Plan includes a commitment to work with Indigenous peoples on IP protection for TK and traditional cultural expressions, so no specific statutory regime exists yet. — [IPIC](https://ipic.ca/news/federal-government-issues-action-plan-for-implementation-of-the-united-nations-declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples-act-2023-06-22). **[UNVERIFIED — background knowledge]** CIPO and ISED run an Indigenous IP program (e.g., Indigenous IP Program, CIPO's Indigenous IP guides). The *Trade-marks Act* s. 9 official marks have been used by First Nations. WIPO adopted a Treaty on IP, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge in May 2024, requiring patent applicants to disclose origin of GR/associated TK. Canada's signature or ratification status is not confirmed.
- **TCPS 2 Art. 9.18.** IP to be discussed and specified in a research agreement. — [TCPS 2 Ch. 9](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter9-chapitre9.html)
- **Indigenous Knowledge Policy Framework for Project Reviews and Regulatory Decisions.** **[UNVERIFIED — background knowledge]** Published by the Impact Assessment Agency of Canada (2022). It governs federal handling of Indigenous Knowledge in project reviews (confidentiality of IK under the *Impact Assessment Act* s. 119). The *Impact Assessment Act* largely does not apply in the Nunavut Settlement Area, where NuPPAA/NIRB govern, so relevance to this project is minimal except as a model of IK confidentiality.

### Inferences
- **Physical samples** are owned or held by whoever lawfully collected them (the university), subject to licence conditions. NRI licences and KivIA permits can add terms (e.g., sample return or reporting).
- **Sequence data and genomes.** No property right is specific to sequences in Canadian law. Copyright in raw data is doubtful (facts are not protected); compiled databases may have thin compilation copyright. Control is therefore contractual and ethical. A community research agreement is the main tool to set Inuit governance (decisions on public deposit, metadata, commercial use, benefit-sharing if commercialized).
- **Names.** SeqCode names are nomenclatural acts, not property. Etymologies citing Inuktitut words and naming credit are attributions. Inuktitut words are not copyrightable. Recordings, written etymology explanations, artwork and handouts produced in the workshop are copyright works (authors are the creators unless assigned), so the agreement should cover licensing these to the project and any Creative Commons terms.
- **Patents or commercialization** from Nunavut microbes (enzymes etc.) would not, under current law, trigger mandatory ABS. Benefit-sharing would rest on voluntary agreement, the Cali Fund norm, or TCPS 9.13/9.18 expectations.

### Gaps
- Canada's position on the 2024 WIPO GRATK Treaty and on the Cali Fund (not verified).
- Any NTI/KivIA policy specifically on genetic resources or biological samples (none found).

## 8. Privacy: participant personal information (naming credits)

### Takeaway
A university's handling of participants' personal information is governed by the privacy law of the university's home province (public-sector FOIP-type statutes) and TCPS 2 Chapter 5. PIPEDA generally does not apply to non-commercial university research. Nunavut's *Access to Information and Protection of Privacy Act* (ATIPP) binds Nunavut public bodies (GN, Hamlets, Nunavut Arctic College/NRI), not out-of-territory universities. Listing names in published etymologies or protologues is disclosure of personal information that needs explicit, informed, revocable-before-publication consent.

### Cited Findings
- NRI: "When communicating your results, it is essential to protect the confidentiality and anonymity of individual participants and to safeguard any sensitive information." It requires translated consent forms. — [NRI guide May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **[UNVERIFIED — background knowledge]** PIPEDA (S.C. 2000, c. 5) applies to private-sector organizations' personal information in commercial activities. Universities are generally not covered except for commercial activities. Nunavut ATIPP (S.N.W.T. 1994, c. 20, as duplicated for Nunavut; amended 2017 to include municipalities, and privacy-breach provisions) applies to Nunavut public bodies. The federal *Privacy Act* applies only to federal institutions. Bill C-27 (Consumer Privacy Protection Act) died on the order paper in January 2025 when Parliament was prorogued.
- TCPS 2 Ch. 5 (privacy and confidentiality) and Art. 9.17/9.18 apply to the workshop. — [TCPS 2 Ch. 9](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter9-chapitre9.html)

### Inferences
- **Practical obligations.** Separate consent tick-boxes for (a) participation, (b) audio/video/photo, (c) being named in publications, SeqCode registry entries or the website, and (d) attribution to an individual versus the community collectively. Respect choices for collective-only credit (e.g., "the people of Igluligaarjuk"). Notify the Nunavut Film Development Corporation if filming (NRI). If a Hamlet or other GN body co-organizes, records it holds become subject to ATIPP.

### Gaps
- Which university and province are involved (determines the governing public-sector privacy statute).
- Current ATIPP Nunavut amendments in 2023–2026 (not checked).
