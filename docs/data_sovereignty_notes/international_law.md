# International legal and treaty frameworks: genetic resources, DSI and Indigenous rights, applied to environmental microbial DNA from Igluligaarjuk (Nunavut)

Notes compiled 29 October 2026. COP17 (Yerevan) was still in session when these notes were written. It closes 30 Oct 2026, so final COP17 DSI outcomes are NOT included. Items marked **[unverified]** come from treaty text or background knowledge that I did not re-fetch in this session. Check them before putting them on slides.

## 1. Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD): Articles 8(j) and 15

### Takeaway
Canada has ratified the CBD and is bound by it. The Convention gives states sovereign rights over their genetic resources (Art. 15) and commits Parties to respect Indigenous knowledge and share its benefits (Art. 8(j)). Canada has no comprehensive national access and benefit-sharing (ABS) law that puts these articles into effect, so they bind Canada as a state but impose no direct legal duty on a university researcher sampling in Nunavut.

### Cited Findings
- Canada is a Party to the CBD and hosts the CBD Secretariat in Montreal. — [ECCC, Nagoya Protocol page](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/corporate/international-affairs/partnerships-organizations/nagoya-protocol-access-genetic-resources.html)
- Canada has no single, comprehensive ABS system. Some federal, provincial and territorial laws cover parts of ABS, and an interdepartmental committee on ABS "has been looking at this issue". (Page last modified 2022-09-02.) — [ECCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/corporate/international-affairs/partnerships-organizations/nagoya-protocol-access-genetic-resources.html)
- **[unverified, standard treaty text]** Art. 8(j) requires each Party, "subject to its national legislation", to respect, preserve and maintain the knowledge, innovations and practices of indigenous and local communities, to promote their wider application with the approval and involvement of the knowledge holders, and to encourage equitable sharing of benefits. Art. 15(1) recognises states' sovereign rights over natural resources and gives national governments the authority to determine access. Art. 15(5) makes access subject to prior informed consent (PIC) of the providing Party. Art. 15(4) requires access on mutually agreed terms (MAT). Art. 15(7) requires fair and equitable benefit-sharing. — [CBD text](https://www.cbd.int/convention/text)
- COP16 (Cali, 2024) created a permanent subsidiary body on Article 8(j). At COP17 (Oct 2026), "arrangements for the new permanent body on Indigenous Peoples and local communities under Article 8(j)" were still unresolved as of 26 Oct 2026. — [CBD press release, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.cbd.int/doc/press/2026/pr-2026-10-26-cop17-en.pdf)
- Academic commentary argues that Nunavut's Scientists Act, Wildlife Act and Article 5 of the Nunavut Agreement leave consent and ABS for genetic resources "unaccounted for". — [Cambridge, *Genetic Resources, Justice and Reconciliation*, ch. "Making room for the Nagoya Protocol in Nunavut"](https://cambridge.org/core/books/genetic-resources-justice-and-reconciliation/making-room-for-the-nagoya-protocol-in-nunavut/E82D412D0E644936444E0B3FC25A2352)
- Precedent: the UPEI Marine Natural Products Lab and Nunavut Tunngavik Inc. negotiated a "potentially precedent setting" ABS agreement for microbial bioprospecting in Nunavut. — [PMC3754551](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3754551)
- Nunavut's Scientists Act requires a licence to do scientific research in Nunavut or to collect specimens there for research. — [NRI, Research Authorizations Nunavut](https://nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/Research_Authorizations_Nunavut.pdf) (domestic law is covered in detail elsewhere)

### Inferences
- Under the CBD, the "providing Party" for these samples is Canada. Inuit rights over the resources come through domestic instruments (the Nunavut Agreement, the UN Declaration Act), not through any federal ABS permit regime.
- Because no federal PIC/MAT regime exists, the only legal "permission" layer is Nunavut research licensing plus any agreement made with the community or the Inuit organisations. A voluntary benefit-sharing and consent agreement is therefore a matter of good practice, not a CBD obligation that falls on the researchers.

- I did not confirm the COP16 decision number for the Art. 8(j) subsidiary body (believed to be decision 16/5) **[unverified]**.

## 2. Nagoya Protocol: is Canada a Party?

### Takeaway
Canada is **not** a Party to the Nagoya Protocol (status confirmed on the ECCC page, last modified Sept 2022; I found no evidence of accession up to Oct 2026). The Protocol's PIC/MAT and Indigenous provisions (Arts. 5, 6(2), 7, 12) therefore do not legally bind Canada. They are still the global benchmark that the community and journals refer to.

### Cited Findings
- "Canada has not ratified this agreement and therefore it is not legally binding on Canada." The Protocol has been in force internationally since 12 Oct 2014. Canada is consulting provinces, territories and Indigenous communities and governments, and will keep examining what accession would mean. — [ECCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/corporate/international-affairs/partnerships-organizations/nagoya-protocol-access-genetic-resources.html)
- Why Canada has not joined: in 2012 the federal government said it would not ratify, citing "lack of preparedness". Indigenous peoples argued the Protocol was negotiated without consulting them and therefore without their consent. Commentators also cite "complexity, political disinterest, entrenched interests, senior bureaucratic inertia", and a failure to see Canada as both a user and a provider of genetic resources. — [Cambridge chapter, ABS Canada initiative](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3C8B5FEA39E4B3CCA3FF9595917040A8/9781108470766c1_3-19.pdf/abs_canada_initiative.pdf); [ABS Canada, "Leader turned laggard"](https://abs-canada.org/leader-turned-laggard-canada-the-nagoya-protocol-and-the-road-ahead)
- No federal or provincial legislation has been adopted to implement the Protocol's subject matter. — [Mondaq/Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/life-sciences-biotechnology-nanotechnology/355234/canadian-perspective-on-legal-issues-and-challenges-facing-the-use-of-genetic-resources-under-the-nagoya-protocol)
- Party count: the CBD signatories page lists 143 Parties. Armenia ratified on 8 June 2026, so the count is current to at least mid-2026. — [CBD Nagoya signatories](https://www.cbd.int/abs/nagoya-protocol/signatories/). WIPO Lex lists 141, probably an older count. — [WIPO Lex](https://cdn.nestjs.wipolex.wji.prd.web1.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/parties/311)
- NP-MOP6 met alongside COP17 (Yerevan, 19–30 Oct 2026). Two Nagoya decisions, on monitoring and reporting and on compliance, had been adopted by 26 Oct 2026. — [CBD press release 26 Oct 2026](https://www.cbd.int/doc/press/2026/pr-2026-10-26-cop17-en.pdf)
- **[unverified, standard treaty text]** Protocol provisions: Art. 5(2): benefits from genetic resources held by indigenous and local communities (ILCs) are shared with them, consistent with domestic law on their established rights. Art. 6(2): Parties must ensure PIC or "approval and involvement" of ILCs for access to genetic resources where those communities have the established right to grant access. Art. 7: access to traditional knowledge (TK) associated with genetic resources needs ILC PIC or approval and involvement, plus MAT. Art. 12: Parties take ILC customary laws, community protocols and procedures into account, and support community development of protocols and model contractual clauses. Art. 8(a): simplified access for non-commercial research. Art. 17: checkpoints and internationally recognised certificates of compliance. — [Nagoya Protocol text](https://www.cbd.int/abs/doc/protocol/nagoya-protocol-en.pdf)
- After COP16, the DSI Scientific Network advises that physical access to genetic resources and associated TK is still governed by national bilateral ABS systems, so researchers must follow the provider country's rules and permit conditions. — [Muñoz-García & Scholz 2025, *Patterns* 6(3):101208](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11963070/)

### Inferences
- In practice there is no Nagoya permit or certificate to obtain, and the ABS Clearing-House has no Canadian checkpoint. Journals or collaborators (especially EU-based ones under EU Reg. 511/2014) may ask for a "Nagoya statement". The correct answer is "samples collected in Canada; Canada is not a Party to the Nagoya Protocol; collected under Nunavut research licence #… with community consent".
- Arts. 6(2), 7 and 12 are a strong moral and normative template for a community agreement: PIC, MAT, respect for community protocols. They also fit Inuit governance through the Nunavut Agreement. Recommend presenting them as "best practice the world agreed on", not as Canadian law.
- If Canada accedes later, whether Inuit have an "established right to grant access" to microbes would depend on the Nunavut Agreement and domestic law. That question is unresolved.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm any change in Canada's position between 2022 and 2026, for example a COP17 statement.

## 3. Digital sequence information: COP15 decision 15/9, COP16 decision 16/2 (Cali Fund), status in Oct 2026, COP17

### Takeaway
Decision 16/2 set up a voluntary, "decoupled" multilateral benefit-sharing mechanism for DSI. Large commercial users "should" pay 1% of profits or 0.1% of revenue into the Cali Fund. Public databases and academic institutions are **not expected to pay**, and at least half of the fund should go to Indigenous peoples and local communities (IPLCs), including those in developed countries "where appropriate". Uptake is tiny: deposits were about US$9,000 in late Oct 2026. COP17 is negotiating who must contribute, certificates, and database accountability, with final decisions due on 30 Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Decision 16/2 (adopted 1 Nov 2024, CBD/COP/DEC/16/2), [text](https://www.cbd.int/doc/decisions/cop-16/cop-16-dec-02-en.pdf):**
- Para 3: entities exceeding two of three thresholds (total assets US$20M, sales US$50M, profit US$5M, averaged over 3 years) "should contribute … 1 per cent of their profits or 0.1 per cent of their revenue, as an indicative rate". Para 4: COP17 is to establish thresholds and rates.
- Para 9: "Entities operating public databases and public research and academic institutions are not expected to make monetary contributions to the global fund."
- Paras 6–7: all users "should share non-monetary benefits", including capacity-building for "generation of, access to and use and storage of" DSI and the "self-identified needs of indigenous peoples and local communities".
- Para 10: databases that make DSI publicly available "should": (a) inform users about the mechanism; (b) inform submitters "of the requirement to comply with applicable national and international access and benefit-sharing obligations"; (c) "require the provision of information on the country of origin … where known", plus, when appropriate, metadata including "an indication of the use of traditional knowledge … and its origin or source"; (d) follow data governance consistent with open access and with the FAIR, **CARE** and TRUST principles and the UNESCO Open Science Recommendation; (e) ask submitters to indicate that the data is "not subject to any restrictions that prohibit its sharing".
- Para 11: Parties funding or hosting sequence databases "should ensure" that the databases implement the decision. Para 12 "encourages" the same of non-Party governments, which matters for the US/NCBI.
- Para 18: funding is also "available … to indigenous peoples and local communities in developed countries, where appropriate".
- Para 21: "at least half of the funding … should support the self-identified needs of indigenous peoples and local communities … through government authorities or by direct payments through institutions identified by indigenous peoples and local communities." This is qualified by "where appropriate, and subject to national circumstances".
- Para 23: the fund is administered by the UN Multi-Partner Trust Fund Office. Para 29: the mechanism's effectiveness is reviewed at COP18, using a methodology to be adopted at COP17.
- Enclosure I lists sectors: pharma, nutraceuticals, cosmetics, breeding, biotech, sequencing lab equipment, and "information, scientific and technical services … including artificial intelligence".
- Enclosure II: one allocation criterion is the "geographical origin of the genetic resources from which [DSI] in the database was derived (noting that such data … are currently often incomplete)".

**Cali Fund status:**
- Launched in Rome on 25 Feb 2025 at the resumed COP16. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/?p=60573); [CBD, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.cbd.int/article/2026-CaliFund)
- First contribution: US$1,000 from UK start-up TierraViva AI, announced 19 Nov 2025. — [Carbon Brief / Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/cali-fund-aiming-to-raise-billions-for-nature-receives-first-donation-of-just-1000)
- Website https://thecalifund.org/ launched 4 Mar 2026. Financial information is on the MPTFO fund page (mptf.undp.org/fund/cal00). — [CBD](https://www.cbd.int/article/2026-CaliFund)
- As of mid-Oct 2026 there were "only two contributions, totalling $6,000". The Grantham Institute estimates the potential at US$3.6bn/yr (all 21,690 eligible firms) or about US$929M/yr under realistic uptake. "No government has passed a law requiring companies to contribute" (Cali Watch). — [Carbon Brief, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop17-developed-countries-fall-far-short-of-un-targets-for-nature-finance)
- Reported totals conflict. Earth Negotiations Bulletin (via The Climate Watch, 28 Oct 2026): deposits of **US$8,990** as of 26 Oct talks. A WWF figure of US$10,656 committed from five companies was also reported. — [The Climate Watch, 28 Oct 2026](https://theclimatewatch.com/cop17-finance-divisions-persist-as-cali-fund-holds-just-8990/); [search summary of Dhaka Tribune/WWF reports](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/bangladesh/foreign-affairs/422893/cop17-faces-crunch-talks-over-biodiversity) (WWF figure not independently verified)
- The IPLC lead on the Cali Fund steering committee is Dr June Rubis. — [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop17-developed-countries-fall-far-short-of-un-targets-for-nature-finance)

**COP17 (Yerevan, Armenia, 19–30 Oct 2026). Status as of 26–28 Oct 2026, final outcome pending:**
- Unresolved as of 26 Oct: "which commercial users of digital sequence information should contribute to the Cali Fund", and arrangements for the new permanent Article 8(j) body. Fourteen decisions had been adopted by then (4 COP, 8 Cartagena, 2 Nagoya). — [CBD press release, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.cbd.int/doc/press/2026/pr-2026-10-26-cop17-en.pdf)
- Certificates: developing countries such as Brazil, Egypt and Malawi want certificates only for payments at the full indicative rate. The EU, Australia and Switzerland warned that strict conditions deter business. Delegates deleted text making access to Cali Fund resources conditional on recognising certificates. Parties are split on whether open access to data counts as a benefit. The DSI contact group is co-chaired by Patience Gandiwa (Zimbabwe) and Helmut Gaugitsch (Austria). — [The Climate Watch, 28 Oct 2026](https://theclimatewatch.com/cop17-finance-divisions-persist-as-cali-fund-holds-just-8990/)
- The leaders' Yerevan Declaration called for stepping up fair and equitable sharing of DSI benefits, including through the Cali Fund. — [Armenpress](https://armenpress.am/en/article/1263055) (search-summary level)
- Database accountability: SBI-7 considered a study on new tools and models (such as databases) for making DSI available transparently. According to TWN, the study dismissed most proposals and recommended continued engagement with IPLCs and scientists to align databases with CARE. The COP17 draft (paras 37–41) covers awareness materials, better provenance and metadata, capacity-building and reporting. The African Group wants at least one accountable, transparent DSI database. TWN reports that only about 51.9M of about 307M INSDC records (~17%) carry geographic collection information, and that "INSDC databases generally lack the verification tools" to establish lawful access. — [TWN Briefing, 17 Oct 2026](https://twn.my/title2/biotk/2026/btk261005.htm) (advocacy NGO, so treat its framing accordingly)

**What this means for academic researchers:**
- DSI Scientific Network: no ABS permit is needed to download and use open-access DSI. Non-commercial users are not required to pay but "may contribute voluntarily" and are expected to share non-monetary benefits. Deposit is fine unless permit terms restrict it. Submitters must state the country of origin. New TK metadata indication. National clauses attached to individual public records "would conflict with the multilateral mechanism". — [Muñoz-García & Hartman Scholz 2025, *Patterns*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11963070/)

### Inferences
- For this project: depositing MAGs in INSDC (required by SeqCode) carries **no Cali Fund payment duty** for the university (para 9). The relevant expectations are (i) accurate country-of-origin metadata (Canada, plus location detail), (ii) indicating any use of TK, and (iii) non-monetary benefit-sharing with the community: training, capacity-building and co-authorship.
- Para 18 + para 21 mean Inuit organisations could in principle seek Cali Fund money as IPLCs in a developed country "where appropriate". With roughly US$9k in the fund, this has no practical value in 2026. It is still worth explaining to the community as a mechanism that recognises them.
- The decoupled model is a trade-off for communities. Once sequences are public, downstream commercial users owe benefits to a global fund, **not** to Igluligaarjuk. Any community-specific benefit has to be negotiated *before* deposit (bilaterally), or secured through non-monetary means: naming, credit, labels, capacity. This is a key slide message.
- Para 10(e) and the Scholz commentary suggest that attaching use restrictions to public records is discouraged under the mechanism. Community wishes therefore have to be expressed through metadata and labels (notices, provenance), not through access restrictions, *or* by choosing not to deposit particular data.

### Gaps
- Final COP17 decisions on DSI (thresholds and rates, certificates, review methodology, allocation formula, database measures) were not available on 29 Oct 2026. Check ENB (enb.iisd.org) and cbd.int/decisions/cop-17 after 30 Oct 2026.

- Canada's negotiating position on DSI at COP17 was not found.

## 4. Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (KMGBF): Targets 13 and 22 (and 21)

### Takeaway
The KMGBF (decision 15/4, Dec 2022, adopted in Montreal under the Chinese presidency) is non-binding but adopted by consensus, Canada included. Target 13 commits to significantly increase benefit-sharing, including from DSI, by 2030. Target 22 (with Target 21) commits to full IPLC participation, respect for their rights over lands, territories, resources and TK, and access to TK only with free, prior and informed consent (FPIC). Canada's national biodiversity strategy reports against these targets.

### Cited Findings
- **[unverified, standard text]** Target 13: take legal, policy, administrative and capacity-building measures to ensure fair and equitable benefit-sharing from genetic resources and DSI, and from associated TK, and facilitate appropriate access, "significantly increasing the benefits shared" by 2030, in accordance with applicable international ABS instruments. Target 21: TK of IPLCs should only be accessed with their free, prior and informed consent, in accordance with national legislation. Target 22: ensure full, equitable, inclusive, effective and gender-responsive representation and participation of IPLCs in decision-making and access to justice and information, respecting their cultures and their rights over lands, territories, resources and traditional knowledge. — [KMGBF, CBD/COP/DEC/15/4](https://www.cbd.int/doc/decisions/cop-15/cop-15-dec-04-en.pdf); [Targets page](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/targets)
- Decision 16/2 para 30: the DSI mechanism's review uses the headline indicators for Goal C and Target 13, plus a binary indicator for Target 13. — [Decision 16/2](https://www.cbd.int/doc/decisions/cop-16/cop-16-dec-02-en.pdf)
- COP17 is running the first global review of KMGBF progress. The UN's State of Biodiversity Action report found "substantial gaps in ambition and delivery". — [CBD press release 26 Oct 2026](https://www.cbd.int/doc/press/2026/pr-2026-10-26-cop17-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- Targets 21 and 22 give the workshop a direct international basis. Community choice of Inuktitut names and the recording of Inuit knowledge about places and organisms are forms of IPLC participation and of control over TK. If Inuit knowledge feeds etymologies or descriptions, FPIC applies in spirit.

## 5. WIPO Treaty on IP, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge (GRATK, 2024)

### Takeaway
Adopted 24 May 2024. It requires patent applicants to disclose the country of origin or source of genetic resources and the Indigenous source of associated TK. As of 8 July 2026 it is **not in force**: 4 of the 15 required ratifications. **Canada did not sign** (signature closed 23 May 2025) and has not acceded. It covers patents only, and DSI is explicitly left to a future review.

### Cited Findings
- WIPO status, 8 July 2026: 44 signatories (Canada is **not** among them); ratifications/accessions: Albania, Malawi, Peru, Uganda (4). — [WIPO status PDF](https://www.wipo.int/export/sites/www/treaties/en/docs/pdf/gratk.pdf)
- Peru deposited its ratification on 8 July 2026. — [EU IP Helpdesk, 24 Jul 2026](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/peru-ratifies-wipo-treaty-intellectual-property-genetic-resources-and-traditional-knowledge-2026-07-24_en)
- Content: the requirement applies where the claimed invention is "based on" genetic resources or associated TK. Applicants disclose the country of origin (or source) of the genetic resources and the Indigenous peoples or local community that provided the TK (or source), or declare that it is unknown. Patent offices do not verify disclosures. No revocation for non-disclosure alone, except in cases of fraud. Not retroactive. Enters into force 3 months after 15 ratifications. A review 4 years after entry into force may consider "derivatives" and "new and emerging technologies". — [WIPO summary](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/treaties/ip/gratk/summary_gratk)
- Canada was among the member states that agreed to the treaty text by consensus in May 2024. — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2024/06/A-New-International-Treaty-on-Intellectual-Property-and-Indigenous-Knowledge)
- The UN Declaration Act Action Plan (Shared Priorities ch., measure 1.101 **[number unverified]**) commits to aligning Canada's IP laws (Copyright, Trademarks and Patent Acts) with UNDRIP. — [IPIC summary, 22 Jun 2023](https://ipic.ca/news/federal-government-issues-action-plan-for-implementation-of-the-united-nations-declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples-act-2023-06-22)

### Inferences
- The treaty has no direct effect on this project now. If a company later patents an enzyme found in an Igluligaarjuk MAG and files in a GRATK Party, it would have to disclose the "source", for example Canada or the INSDC record. Good provenance metadata (geo_loc_name, BioSample attributes, any labels) is what makes that disclosure traceable to the community.

### Gaps
- No ratification events after 8 July 2026 were found.

## 6. UNDRIP and Canada's UN Declaration Act (brief, international context)

### Takeaway
UNDRIP (UNGA res. 61/295, 2007) is a non-binding declaration. Canada removed its objector status and endorsed it fully in 2016, and gave it a legislated implementation framework in the *United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act* (S.C. 2021, c. 14; Action Plan June 2023, 181 measures). Art. 31 expressly covers Indigenous rights over "genetic resources".

### Cited Findings
- **[unverified, standard text]** UNDRIP Art. 31(1): Indigenous peoples have the right to maintain, control, protect and develop their cultural heritage, TK and traditional cultural expressions, "as well as the manifestations of their sciences, technologies and cultures, including human and genetic resources, seeds, medicines, knowledge of the properties of fauna and flora…", and their intellectual property over these. Art. 31(2) requires states to take effective measures, in conjunction with Indigenous peoples. Art. 18 and Art. 19 (FPIC) are also relevant. — [UNDRIP text](https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/wp-content/uploads/sites/19/2018/11/UNDRIP_E_web.pdf)
- The UN Declaration Act Action Plan was released 21 Jun 2023 with 181 measures over 5 years. — [IPIC](https://ipic.ca/news/federal-government-issues-action-plan-for-implementation-of-the-united-nations-declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples-act-2023-06-22); [Justice Canada Action Plan](https://justice.gc.ca/eng/declaration/ap-pa/guide/index.html)
- **[unverified]** The Act (S.C. 2021, c. 14) received Royal Assent on 21 Jun 2021. Canada endorsed UNDRIP "without qualification" on 10 May 2016. — [Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/U-2.2/)

### Inferences
- Art. 31's explicit mention of "genetic resources" supports the community's claim to a say over microbial genomes from their lands and waters, even though the federal government has not translated this into an ABS regime. This should be a key slide.

### Gaps
- Domestic detail is left to the other researcher. I found no Action Plan measure that names "genetic resources" specifically; the IP measure is the closest.

## 7. BBNJ Agreement (High Seas Treaty)

### Takeaway
The BBNJ Agreement entered into force on 17 Jan 2026. It applies only to areas beyond national jurisdiction (ABNJ), so Chesterfield Inlet and Hudson Bay waters (Canadian internal waters/EEZ) are out of scope. It is still the first binding treaty to regulate marine genetic resources and their DSI, and it sets norms: notification, standardized identifiers, open-access repositories, and Indigenous TK only with consent. Canada signed but had not ratified as of about March 2026; no later confirmation was found.

### Cited Findings
- 60 ratifications were reached on 19 Sept 2025, with Morocco as the 60th. The Agreement entered into force on 17 Jan 2026. — [UN Geneva](https://www.ungeneva.org/en/news-media/news/2025/09/110787/un-high-seas-treaty-clears-ratification-threshold-enter-force); [IISD SDG](https://sdg.iisd.org/news/bbnj-agreement-enters-into-force/)
- 86 Parties as of 24 Mar 2026 (law-firm summary). Wikipedia shows 101 (date unclear). — [Nat Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/high-seas-treaty-takes-force-what-you-need-know); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Seas_Treaty)
- Canada signed in 2024. DFO said Canada "aims to ratify … as soon as possible" and "work is ongoing", with no timeline (reported Jan–Mar 2026). — [Unpublished/National Observer, 16 Jan 2026](https://unpublished.ca/news-feed-item/2026-01-16/canada-shores-up-commitment-to-protect-high-seas-with-un-treaty-set-to); [Living Oceans](https://livingoceans.org/media/news/hey-canada-its-time-ratify-the-high-seas-treaty)
- The Agreement gives the first internationally agreed definition of marine genetic resources, which includes "microbial" material. It covers DSI but does not define it. — [Commonwealth overview](https://thecommonwealth.org/publications/overview-elements)
- Arts. 11–12 set up a "light touch" notification system, collection and utilisation notifications, a Clearing-House Mechanism, and a "BBNJ standardized batch identifier". — [One Ocean Hub](https://oneoceanhub.org/?p=14514); [IUCN DSI brief](https://iucn.org/node/43362)
- **[unverified, treaty text]** Art. 13: Parties take measures so that TK associated with MGR in ABNJ held by Indigenous peoples and local communities is accessed only with their free, prior and informed consent or approval and involvement, in line with UNDRIP. Art. 14: benefit-sharing (non-monetary, including open access to DSI in repositories; monetary via a special fund, with payments that may follow a modality like the CBD mechanism). Art. 12 requires DSI to be deposited in open-access repositories/databases with the identifier. — [BBNJ text, A/CONF.232/2023/4](https://www.un.org/bbnjagreement/en)
- The first BBNJ COP is due within one year of entry into force, i.e. by Jan 2027. — [IISD SDG](https://sdg.iisd.org/news/bbnj-agreement-enters-into-force/)

### Inferences
- Not directly applicable to this project. Its standardized identifier and Indigenous FPIC norms are a useful model: "even on the high seas, the world agreed sequences should carry provenance tags and Indigenous knowledge needs consent."

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether Canada deposited ratification between Mar and Oct 2026 (check UN Treaty Collection, ch. XXI-10). I did not find the BBNJ COP1 date.

## 8. INSDC position on DSI and open access; provenance metadata; Local Contexts

### Takeaway
INSDC keeps "free and unrestricted access" with no use restrictions. Since 2023 it has required country and collection date for new BioSamples, and since late 2024 for sequence records. The `/country` qualifier was renamed `/geo_loc_name` in 2024. I found no formal INSDC policy statement on the Cali Fund for 2025–2026 and no native NCBI/ENA field for Local Contexts labels. Implementation guidance exists via ERGA/COPO.

### Cited Findings
- INSDC policy: "free and unrestricted access with no use restrictions", and data permanently accessible. — [INSDC policy](https://www.insdc.org/?p=31)
- INSDC 2025 *NAR* paper on broadening membership "to reflect the global research community". — Nucleic Acids Res. 2025;53(D1):D62–D66, doi:10.1093/nar/gkae1058 (via [tagteam feed](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/4353/feed_items/14041954)); [INSDC membership guidance, May 2024](https://insdc.org/?p=579)
- Spatiotemporal minimum standard: country and collection date are mandatory for new BioSamples from end of May 2023 unless a valid "missing:" reporting term applies. Phase II (sequence records) was planned for Dec 2024. Lat/lon is encouraged but not mandatory. — [INSDC 3 Mar 2023](https://insdc.org/?p=309); [ENA docs](https://ena-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/faq/spatiotemporal-metadata.html); [DDBJ 2024 meeting report](https://www.ddbj.nig.ac.jp/activities/insdc_meeting/2024-e.html)
- `/country` was renamed `/geo_loc_name` (effective about June 2024). — [DDBJ](https://www.ddbj.nig.ac.jp/news/en/2024-02-01-e.html)
- Stakeholder joint statement for COP16 (2024) urged keeping DSI "open, free and fair" with no payment at the point of access and no mandatory user registration. — [DNFS joint statement](https://dnfs.de/sites/default/files/2024-07/Maintain%20open%2C%20free%20and%20fair%20access%20to%20DSI%20_%20ENG%20fin__Joint%20Statement_.pdf)
- Local Contexts Biocultural (BC) Labels (applied only by Indigenous communities) and BC Notices (applied by researchers and institutions) are machine-readable and designed to persist as metadata in sample and data repositories, linking communities to genetic resources and DSI. — [Liggins et al., Biocultural Labels Initiative, Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4097637)
- ERGA, with COPO and Local Contexts, published guidance for adding IPLC rights and interests to genomic metadata. — [Local Contexts, ERGA guidance](https://localcontexts.org/?p=3186); [ENRICH policy brief on Indigenous provenance in metadata, 2024](https://localcontexts.org/new-policy-brief-on-recognizing-indigenous-provenance-in-metadata/)
- TWN (Oct 2026) says INSDC lacks tools to verify lawful access or right to publish, and that only about 17% of records carry geographic data. — [TWN](https://twn.my/title2/biotk/2026/btk261005.htm)

### Inferences
- Practical steps for this project's MAG deposits: put a precise `geo_loc_name` (e.g., "Canada: Nunavut, Igluligaarjuk (Chesterfield Inlet)") and lat_lon in the BioSamples. Consider adding a Local Contexts Project ID or BC/TK Notice URL as a custom BioSample attribute or in the BioProject description; INSDC accepts free-text custom attributes **[unverified as a formal practice; ERGA guidance describes approaches]**. Inuktitut names registered under the SeqCode add a cultural provenance layer.
- Because INSDC forbids use restrictions, the community cannot set licence conditions on deposited MAGs. The control point is the decision *before* deposit.

### Gaps
- I found no NCBI- or ENA-specific official documentation on Local Contexts fields. I found no INSDC statement responding to decision 16/2 para 10 (2024–2026). GA4GH was not researched; it is human-genomics-focused and of low relevance.
