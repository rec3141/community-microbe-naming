# Real, public documents and agreements governing Indigenous data, samples, genomic/eDNA data and research relationships: an annotated catalogue for an Igluligaarjuk microbe-naming workshop

Compiled 29 Oct 2026. Every entry below was opened and read unless it is marked **[NOT OPENED]** or **[DESCRIBED ONLY]**. Quotes are verbatim from the downloaded text. Paraphrases are marked "paraphrase", and the Danish-language Greenland law is given in my own English translation, marked "translation".

Document-type codes used: RA = research agreement template; DSA = data-sharing agreement; ABS = access and benefit-sharing law or agreement; MTA = material transfer agreement; PROT = protocol, code or guideline; LIC = licence or permit regime; POL = repository or access policy.

---

## Q1. Which Inuit and Arctic documents could be adapted directly?

### Takeaway
No region publishes a signed Inuit-community research agreement. The texts closest to adoption are the following:
- Memorial University's Indigenous Research Agreement, in its Feb 2024 version, used with the Nunatsiavut Government.
- The NWT CIMP Traditional Knowledge Data-Sharing Agreement template from 2018. It names the IRC research guidelines.
- The ICC's 2022 Circumpolar Inuit Protocols, especially Protocol 7.
- The NRI licence guide (May 2025). This is the binding Nunavut layer, and it explicitly restricts secondary use of samples.
- The Qanuilirpitaa? 2017 governance model, which is described in the literature but whose policy text is available only on request.

### Cited Findings

**1. Memorial University of Newfoundland, "Indigenous Research Agreement" (template, version "Feb 16-24")** (RA)
- Issuer: Memorial University, Research Initiatives and Services (RIS). It was implemented in 2020 and is used by several Indigenous groups, including the Nunatsiavut Government. One group added a co-authorship clause. Sources: [MUN news 2020](https://mun.ca/hss/shared-news-articles/principled-partnerships-memorial-implements-agreement-supporting-indigenous-data-sovereignty.php); [toolbox page](https://mun.ca/research/indigenous-research-at-memorial/toolbox/indigenous-research-agreement/); [PDF](https://mun.ca/research/media/production/memorial/administrative/research/media-library/indigenous/Indigenous%20Research%20Agreement%20Feb%202024.pdf).
- Reuse: the PDF is marked "Template agreement for reference only – Please contact RIS for a usable version – terms subject to change without notice." No open licence is given. [PDF](https://mun.ca/research/media/production/memorial/administrative/research/media-library/indigenous/Indigenous%20Research%20Agreement%20Feb%202024.pdf)
- Ownership. Clause 3 reads: "The ICB will retain ownership of all Indigenous Data … including extracts of it." Clause 9 defines "Indigenous Data" to mean "information, data, artifacts **and samples** collected from the ICB's Indigenous community in the course of the Research". [PDF](https://mun.ca/research/media/production/memorial/administrative/research/media-library/indigenous/Indigenous%20Research%20Agreement%20Feb%202024.pdf)
- Access, return and stop-use. Memorial must "provide the ICB with access to the indigenous Data upon written request". It must also "return any or all of the Indigenous Data to the ICB upon written request" and "cause its researchers to cease using any or all of the Indigenous Data for any purpose upon written request by the ICB". [PDF](https://mun.ca/research/media/production/memorial/administrative/research/media-library/indigenous/Indigenous%20Research%20Agreement%20Feb%202024.pdf)
- Publication review (clause 4). Memorial gives the draft to the ICB, which has "forty-five (45) days … to review the Draft for Indigenous Data". Memorial removes any data flagged by the ICB. If the ICB does not respond, "Memorial may Publish". "Results" are defined as interpretation and analyses, "but not the Indigenous Data itself". [PDF](https://mun.ca/research/media/production/memorial/administrative/research/media-library/indigenous/Indigenous%20Research%20Agreement%20Feb%202024.pdf)
- Term and disputes. Either party may terminate on "ten (10) days' written notice", and clauses 3 to 9 survive termination. NL and federal law govern. The ICB acknowledges that Memorial is subject to ATIPPA, the access-to-information law. [PDF](https://mun.ca/research/media/production/memorial/administrative/research/media-library/indigenous/Indigenous%20Research%20Agreement%20Feb%202024.pdf)

Sample clause: "[community] owns the collective TK data and retains all intellectual property rights (including copyright) … This Agreement constitutes only a license to acquire and use these data products; it does not convey title or rights." [DOCX](https://nwtdiscoveryportal.enr.gov.nt.ca/geoportaldocuments/NWT%20CIMP%20-%20TK%20Sharing%20Agreement%20Template%20-%20October%202018.docx)

[DOCX](https://nwtdiscoveryportal.enr.gov.nt.ca/geoportaldocuments/NWT%20CIMP%20-%20TK%20Sharing%20Agreement%20Template%20-%20October%202018.docx)

The template separates the two: "Publically available information need not be the TK data itself, but reports or documents that discuss, summarize or in other ways refer to the data". [DOCX](https://nwtdiscoveryportal.enr.gov.nt.ca/geoportaldocuments/NWT%20CIMP%20-%20TK%20Sharing%20Agreement%20Template%20-%20October%202018.docx)

The agreement can be renewed "with the written consent of all parties at least sixty (60) days prior to the expiration". [DOCX](https://nwtdiscoveryportal.enr.gov.nt.ca/geoportaldocuments/NWT%20CIMP%20-%20TK%20Sharing%20Agreement%20Template%20-%20October%202018.docx)

- Sources: [ICC PDF](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf); [project page](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/project/circumpolar-inuit-protocols-for-equitable-and-ethical-engagement/). [ICC PDF](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf)
- Protocol 7, access: "Inuit access to all raw information and data collected within Inuit Nunaat should be agreed upon and ensured. Including scientific information and data." This bears directly on environmental sequence data. [ICC PDF](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf)
- Credit and review: "Give credit where credit is due". Also: "Require Inuit review and agreement on all materials and products … review results prior to public release in order to identify incorrect or sensitive information." - Language (Protocol 4): "use plain language, translation and interpretation". Also: "Invest in cultural translation – Hire and engage Inuit". Protocol 7 adds: "Report back in appropriate and accessible language". [ICC PDF](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf)
- Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) "applies to all these directives, including ownership and permission" (Protocol 7, referring to Protocol 1). [ICC PDF](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf)

**4. Nunavut Research Institute, "Obtaining a Research License under Nunavut's Scientists Act: A Guide for Applicants" (May 2025)** (LIC, binding in Nunavut)
- Source: [PDF](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf). A licence is required for research "involving people, air, land, or water", even when the work is done remotely. Consultation takes at least 45 business days, and the agencies consulted include HTOs. Applicants must supply a "translated project summary" and "translated consent forms". [PDF](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- Secondary use of samples: "Unless authorized under your license, you may not share research samples or specimens that you collect with other investigators for research purposes that are outside the scope of your original licensed project. A license amendment or new research license must be obtained for any secondary research or analysis of archived samples". [PDF](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- Reporting: an Annual Summary Report is required, plus "copies of any final publications". Researchers are "expected to share your research results directly with Nunavut agencies and community members", using a communication plan. [PDF](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- Other permits apply. These include "Permission from a Regional Inuit Association (RIA) to access or use Inuit Owned Lands" and Nunavut Water Board authorization "to collect water samples". [PDF](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Igluligaarjuk, the binding layers are the NRI licence (Scientists Act) plus permissions from Kivalliq Inuit Association and Inuit-owned-lands holders, the Nunavut Water Board, and the HTO. An agreement template should therefore sit on top of the licence and cite its terms, especially the NRI ban on secondary sample use without an amendment.
- Most existing templates, including MUN and CIMP, assume data can be returned, destroyed, or stopped "on written request". That is in tension with INSDC deposition, because SeqCode registration requires public genomes and public records cannot be recalled. A template for this project needs an explicit, consented carve-out for genome deposition. The ICC's "access to all raw … scientific information and data" and pre-release review clauses fit this better than destroy-on-expiry clauses do.
- MUN's 45-day review with "silence = consent" is a practical model for reviewing names and manuscripts.

### Gaps
- No public signed Inuit research agreement was found for Nunavut (NRI, NTI, KivIA), Nunavik (the Q2017 DMC policy is on request), Nunatsiavut or the ISR.
- The ITK 2007 PDF was not opened.
- No public Polar Knowledge Canada or ArcticNet data policy with Inuit-specific clauses was checked, for lack of time.
- SmartICE agreements were not found.

## Q2. Which genetic-resource, ABS and genomic-data instruments apply to environmental and microbial DNA?

### Takeaway
Greenland's Act No. 3 of 3 June 2016 is the only Arctic statute found that requires a permit, a benefit-sharing agreement and publication notification for genetic resources, including microorganisms, and it applies to research. Te Nohonga Kaitiaki, the Kākāpō125+ terms and NCIG show working Indigenous governance of non-human and human genomic data. GGBN and MOSAICC give MTA and PIC language designed for microbes and genomic samples.

### Cited Findings

**12. Greenland, "Inatsisartutlov nr. 3 af 3. juni 2016 om udnyttelse af genetiske ressourcer og aktiviteter i forbindelse dermed"** (Act No. 3 of 3 June 2016 on the utilisation of genetic resources) (ABS law)
- Source: [FAOLEX PDF](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/grl201579.pdf), in Danish. This act replaced the earlier Act No. 20 of 20 Nov 2006. Leary 2008 called the 2006 law the first Arctic ABS law ([Polar Record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/763C759048FEC81A93234F8F2AD534F4)).
- Scope (translation of §6): "genetic material" means material "from plants, animals or **microorganisms** … containing functional units of heredity". "Utilisation" means "to carry out research or development on the genetic or biochemical composition", and includes collection, storage, transfer and export. [FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/grl201579.pdf)
- Permit and benefit sharing (translation of §7): utilisation of Greenlandic genetic resources "may only take place pursuant to a utilisation permit" from Naalakkersuisut. Benefits, "including economic and non-economic", may be received only under "a benefit-sharing agreement with Naalakkersuisut". §11(2) allows Naalakkersuisut to take co-ownership of IP. §9(3) states that permits carry no exclusive right. [FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/grl201579.pdf)
- Publication (translation of §18): anyone publishing results must notify Naalakkersuisut "immediately after publication". Publications must reference "that the genetic resources originate in Greenland", and copies must be sent to Naalakkersuisut. [FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/grl201579.pdf)
- Traditional knowledge: §5 extends the Act to traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources. §2(2) requires interpretation consistent with "the rights of the Greenlandic people as a people and indigenous people under international law" (translation). [FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/grl201579.pdf)
- 2026 update: a draft executive order, out for consultation in Feb 2026, would set an application fee of DKK 8,000 and a 6-week lead time, with a proposed entry into force of 1 April 2026. Source: [draft PDF](https://naalakkersuisut.gl/-/media/horinger/2026/02/1002_genetisk_ressourcer/dk-udkast-til-selvstyrets-bekendtgrelse-om-myndighedsbehandling-af-genetiske-ressourcer.pdf). Whether it was adopted is not verified.

**13. Te Kotahi Research Institute (Univ. of Waikato), "Te Nohonga Kaitiaki Guidelines for Genomic Research on Taonga Species" (Sept 2021)** (PROT plus checklist)
- Authors listed on the title page: Hudson, Thompson, Battershill and Stott. Stott is a microbiologist. - Objectives include "To support Māori data sovereignty over data generated from research" and "To address the need for benefit-sharing arrangements in compliance with … the Nagoya Protocol". [PDF](https://sites.massey.ac.nz/teaurangahau/wp-content/uploads/sites/53/2021/10/TNKGuidelines.pdf)

  - "Engaging party should absorb cost of engagement"
  - "Engagement is conducted face to face (kanohi ki te kanohi)"
  - "Is there a protocol around publications?"
  - "Rights to intellectual property are well defined"
  - "Are Māori involved in decisions about access and use of new data?"
  
  Genomics Aotearoa reportedly requires new environmental projects to consult the checklist. [PDF](https://sites.massey.ac.nz/teaurangahau/wp-content/uploads/sites/53/2021/10/TNKGuidelines.pdf)

**21. Alpaslan-Roodenberg et al., "Ethics of DNA research on human remains: five globally applicable guidelines", *Nature* 599:41–46 (2021)** (PROT; a counterpoint)
- Source: [PMC7612683](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612683/). Guideline 4: "Researchers must ensure that data are made available following publication". Restricted or committee-controlled access is acceptable, but "no stakeholder group … should control the distribution of data to researchers" who wish to re-examine findings. Guideline 5: "Researchers must accept a negative answer if stakeholders are not collectively supportive". The paper rejects stakeholder sign-off before publication as "not feasible". [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612683/)

**22. Local Contexts Biocultural (BC) Labels and Notices; ERGA/COPO implementation guidance** (labels)
- BC Notices are applied by researchers or institutions, and their text "cannot be changed or customized". Labels are applied by communities through the Local Contexts Hub. Sources: [Local Contexts BC Notice](https://localcontexts.org/?p=1595); [ERGA guidance page](https://localcontexts.org/guidance-documentation-for-implementing-the-traditional-knowledge-and-biocultural-labels-in-the-european-reference-genome-atlas/).
- ERGA created metadata fields for Labels and Notices in biodiversity genome submissions. [ERGA guidance page](https://localcontexts.org/guidance-documentation-for-implementing-the-traditional-knowledge-and-biocultural-labels-in-the-european-reference-genome-atlas/)

### Inferences
- If any comparison material or collaborators come from Greenland, the 2016 Act is the clearest "real law" exhibit. It shows an Inuit-majority government asserting sovereign rights over microbial DNA, requiring notification of publications and attribution of origin, and reserving IP co-ownership.
- The Kākāpō125+ terms are the best model for public deposition combined with Indigenous control. They let data go into public databases when journals require it, while banning onward transfer and unauthorised commercial use, and they require pre-publication sharing with the iwi. The same structure could govern Chesterfield Inlet MAGs: raw reads and genomes go to INSDC, while metadata, names and stories stay under community control. BC Notices or Labels in INSDC or BioProject metadata would provide provenance.
- The Nature 2021 guidelines show the scientific-community pushback against community veto over published data. Expect reviewers to raise this.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Denmark's Nagoya ratification extends to Greenland, or whether the 2026 fee order was adopted.
- No example Nagoya IRCC for microbes was retrieved. One ABSCH search failed with a tool error, and another returned only South African marine IRCCs.
- I did not verify Canada's Nagoya status. To my knowledge Canada is not a Party, so no Canadian IRCC would exist, but this was not sourced here.
- The current AGDR access agreement, the Native BioData Consortium data-use agreements ([nativebio.org](https://www.nativebio.org/about-us) describes attorneys drafting DSAs but publishes no text), and the NCIG Ethics Protocol text were not obtained.

## Q3. Which other First Nations, Canadian and global Indigenous templates and codes are adaptable?

### Takeaway
These are useful clause sources:
- the Alberta FNIGC DSA framework (return/destruction, IP and publication with "right of dissent");
- NSERC's new Indigenous Engagement and Collaboration Agreement Template (IECAT), a funder form for the 2027 competition year;
- Mi'kmaw Ethics Watch (collective ownership, return to the community);
- the San Code of Research Ethics 2017 (honesty, benefits, sanctions);
- the Charles Darwin University research agreement template (a checklist of headings).

### Cited Findings

**23. Alberta First Nations Information Governance Centre, "Framework for a Data Sharing Agreement" (prepared by Krista Yao, Nadjiwan Law; undated)** (DSA template with drafting notes)
- Source: [PDF](https://docs.fntn.ca/VC11512/Handouts/Data-Sharing-Agreement.pdf). It describes itself as a "community template … a template or guideline of considerations", noting that "we cannot have a single template that will meet all situations". [PDF](https://docs.fntn.ca/VC11512/Handouts/Data-Sharing-Agreement.pdf)
- Where research is involved, "you will have to include provisions for pre-publication review by First Nation parties". Clause 14 covers IP, copyright and publication: "Typically, First Nation parties would retain ownership of data that they have contributed". It calls for "pre-publication notices with the right of dissent, and correct attribution of First Nation data". [PDF](https://docs.fntn.ca/VC11512/Handouts/Data-Sharing-Agreement.pdf)
- Clause 13 (return or destruction): "The Data Steward shall send a letter to the Parties confirming that the destruction/return of data has been completed". [PDF](https://docs.fntn.ca/VC11512/Handouts/Data-Sharing-Agreement.pdf)
- Clause 16 (general): terms on "creation, use, disclosure and destruction of First Nations Data … shall survive the termination". No assignment is allowed, and amendment requires mutual written agreement. [PDF](https://docs.fntn.ca/VC11512/Handouts/Data-Sharing-Agreement.pdf)

**24. Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC), "Indigenous Engagement and Collaboration Agreement Template" (IECAT; posted 2026-06; file for the "CY2027" competition year)** (funder form, signed by the community partner)
- Source: [DOCX](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/IECAT-GAPCA_CY2027_e.docx). Researchers must "Respect and uphold Indigenous data sovereignty, including community control over ownership, access and future data use". They must also "Acknowledge community contributions through co-authorship, shared publications or benefit-sharing". [DOCX](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/IECAT-GAPCA_CY2027_e.docx)
- "Recognize that consent and authorization, including free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) where applicable, are ongoing and may need to be revisited if the scope, location, methods, data or **sample use** … changes." [DOCX](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/IECAT-GAPCA_CY2027_e.docx)
- Applicants must cover permits for "land- and/or water-based research activities". Section 2 is completed by "the authorized representative of the Indigenous community partner", with tick-boxes such as "Data ownership, governance, access or future use". [DOCX](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/IECAT-GAPCA_CY2027_e.docx)

**25. Mi'kmaw Ethics Watch (Sante' Mawio'mi, 1999/2000; now under the Union of Nova Scotia Mi'kmaq), "Mi'kmaw Research Principles and Protocols"** (PROT)
- Source: [PDF](https://www.cbu.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/MEW-Principles-and-Protocols.pdf). The document opens with text in Mi'kmaw. Its purpose is to "guarantee that the right of ownership rests with the various Mi'kmaw communities". "Interpretation and conclusions drawn from the research will be subject to approval". [PDF](https://www.cbu.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/MEW-Principles-and-Protocols.pdf)
- "Mi'kmaw knowledge is collectively owned … and so also must be collectively guarded." "All research … belongs to the community and must be returned to that community." Research is "a negotiated partnership", and participants are "equals … instead of as 'informants' or 'subjects'". [PDF](https://www.cbu.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/MEW-Principles-and-Protocols.pdf)
- A 2023 toolkit is also available: [PDF](https://www.cbu.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/MEW-Toolkit_2023_Final.pdf).

**26. South African San Council and South African San Institute, "San Code of Research Ethics" (launched 2 Mar 2017)** (PROT)
- Source: [English PDF](https://www.globalcodeofconduct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/San-Code-of-RESEARCH-Ethics-Booklet_English.pdf); [GCC page](https://www.globalcodeofconduct.org/affiliated-codes/). Its sections are Respect, Honesty, Justice and Fairness, Care, and Process. [English PDF](https://www.globalcodeofconduct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/San-Code-of-RESEARCH-Ethics-Booklet_English.pdf)
- "The language must be clear, not academic." It describes past failures to "show the San the research prior to publication". It also condemns "exaggerated claims of the researcher's lack of resources, and thus the researchers' inability to provide any benefits". [English PDF](https://www.globalcodeofconduct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/San-Code-of-RESEARCH-Ethics-Booklet_English.pdf)
- Benefits "might be largely non-monetary but include co-research opportunities, sharing of skills and research capacity, and roles for translators and research assistants". [English PDF](https://www.globalcodeofconduct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/San-Code-of-RESEARCH-Ethics-Booklet_English.pdf)
- Enforcement: "dispute resolution mechanisms", a possible "black book", and refusal of future collaboration with institutions that do not comply. The code closes: "come through the door, not the window". [English PDF](https://www.globalcodeofconduct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/San-Code-of-RESEARCH-Ethics-Booklet_English.pdf)

**27. Benefit-sharing agreements from South Africa** **[DESCRIBED ONLY; texts not public]**
- Hoodia (24 Mar 2003, San Council and CSIR). The San receive 8% of milestone payments and 6% of royalties, paid into the San Hoodia Benefit Sharing Trust. Source: [Cultural Survival](https://cs.org/node/6542).
- Rooibos (2019; SA Rooibos Council, National Khoi-San Council and SA San Council). A 1.5% farm-gate levy goes to a bioprospecting trust and is split equally between the two councils, with annual review. Source: [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/landmark-deal-to-give-khoi-and-san-benefit-sharing-levy-in-rooibos-2019-11-01).

**28. Charles Darwin University (Australia), "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research Agreement Guidance Template" (2019)** (RA headings)
- Source: [DOCX](https://www.cdu.edu.au/files/2021-03/atsira_template_2019%20%282%29.docx). It follows NHMRC 2018 guidance. Required headings include "Ownership (copyright and intellectual property management); distribution and storage of all data collected" and "Conflict resolution and complaint processes". Commitments include "acknowledgement, authorship". [DOCX](https://www.cdu.edu.au/files/2021-03/atsira_template_2019%20%282%29.docx)

**29. Kahnawake Schools Diabetes Prevention Project (KSDPP), "Code of Research Ethics" (1995/1996; revised 2005–07)** **[DESCRIBED ONLY; code text not opened]**
- The code has sections on principles, the obligations of community and academic researchers, and the rights of the community and participants. Sources: [PMC6990238](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6990238); [McGill slides](https://www.mcgill.ca/healthlaw/files/healthlaw/Macaulay_Law_and_Medicine_Nov2010.pdf).

**30. Government of Canada DMP guidance (Tri-agency RDM) and the Alliance Simplified DMP Template** (DMP)
- "For research conducted by and with First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities, DMPs should be co-developed with these communities", citing CARE, OCAP® and NISR. Source: [science.gc.ca](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/node/1406). No Inuit-specific DMP Assistant template was found.

### Inferences
- A workable Igluligaarjuk template could draw on these sources:
  - the MUN agreement for the structure (ownership of data and samples, access, publication review, term);
  - the CIMP template for the "licence, not title" wording and the instruction to renegotiate any new use;
  - the AFNIGC framework for a destruction certificate and a "right of dissent";
  - ICC Protocol 7 and NISR for Inuktut and reporting-back clauses;
  - the NSERC IECAT, because it is likely to be required by the funder anyway;
  - the Kākāpō terms for the carve-out allowing public-database deposition.
- None of the templates found grants an explicit open licence such as CC-BY. Adapting text should be presented as "modelled on", with attribution, and permission requested where needed (for example, MUN says to contact RIS).

### Gaps
- FNIGC's own national DSA template was not found publicly. The FNC "Considerations and Templates" (2007) PDF has moved and was not opened.
- The CIHR 2007 Guidelines' sample agreement language was not retrieved.
- The text of the Six Nations/Manitoulin codes and SING Canada documents was not checked.

## Q4. Are there published community agreements from microbiome or eDNA studies with Indigenous communities?

### Takeaway
I found no publicly posted, signed agreement from an Indigenous microbiome study. The literature describes frameworks, not agreement texts.

### Cited Findings
- Bader et al. (2023, *Nature Microbiology*) propose a relational framework for microbiome research with Indigenous communities. Partners should hold a central role in setting research questions, designing consent and data-management protocols, and interpreting results. Sources: [Penn State Q&A](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/qa-indigenous-community-first-approach-more-ethical-microbiome-research); [McGill news](https://mcgill.ca/newsroom/node/307410).
- A companion Perspective (Handsley-Davis et al.) describes "microbiome ownership" and benefit flowing mostly to non-Indigenous researchers. It cites a Peru case where work expanded beyond the authorised scope to gut microbiome profiling. Source: [News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20231027/Ethical-microbiome-research-with-Indigenous-communities-A-framework-for-collaboration-and-sovereignty.aspx).
- A Canadian H. pylori genomics project with NWT and Yukon communities is examining policies on sharing microbial genomic data from Indigenous partnerships. No agreement text was found. Source: [Genome Alberta](https://dev.genomealberta.ca/?p=5714).

### Inferences
- The Peru example matches the NRI rule against secondary use of samples. It is a concrete warning for the workshop: amplicon or MAG data reused for unrelated questions needs fresh permission.

### Gaps
- No signed agreement texts were found for the Hadza, Matses or US tribal microbiome studies. The ancient-DNA community agreements are described only in papers.
- The Native BioData Consortium does not publish its DSAs.
