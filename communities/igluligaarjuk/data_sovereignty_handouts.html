Our waters, our say

We took water from the inlet and the lakes. We read the DNA of the microbes in it, and we are now giving some of them names in Inuktitut. Before we share anything, we want to agree together on who decides what. This is called data sovereignty.

What Inuit organizations have said

  • Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami (2018): "Ensure Inuit access, ownership, and control over data and information."
  • Inuit Circumpolar Council (2022): this includes "scientific information and data" collected on Inuit lands and waters. Inuit should review results before they are made public.

The law and research rules

  • No law says who owns microbe DNA.
  • Research rules say the work must benefit the community, and ownership should be written in an agreement.
  • We will write one short agreement with the HTO.

Three ways to answer

Yes
Yes, with conditions
No

We wait for a clear answer; silence is not taken as a yes. You can take part and still say no to photos, recordings or having your name used.

Making a name official

To make a name official, the rules for naming microbes (the SeqCode) say that microbe's genome is shared in the world's public DNA library. Once it is shared, scientists anywhere can use it, and it stays there.

Stays with us

  • Water samples and DNA
  • Genomes we are not naming
  • Exact sampling spots
  • The stories behind the names

Shared publicly, after the community says yes

  • The genome of each named microbe
  • Its name and what the name means
  • Only the location details we agree to

What the community gets

  • Credit in every name
  • Pay for Elders, knowledge holders and interpreters
  • Local jobs and training for youth
  • Results brought back here, in Inuktitut
  • Anyone who wants to make money from these genomes must come to the community first

What our research licence promises

  • A report every year, in Inuktitut and English
  • No new use of the samples without permission
  • No giving samples to anyone outside the project
  • We come back to share the results

Six decisions

📝 First draft. Tick the option you like. Today's choices become final only when we sign a written agreement with the HTO. ★ marks our suggestion.

1After the workshop, who makes these decisions?

A. The HTO board alone. Already reviews research here, but Elders and youth may not have a voice.
B. A data group led by the HTO, with an Elder, a youth and someone from the Hamlet. ★ Why: the HTO keeps the lead, and Elders and youth are part of every decision.
C. A public meeting every time. Everyone gets a say, but it is hard to meet for each decision.

2When does a genome go public?

A. Post all of them now. Fastest, but the community gives up its say before the names are chosen.
B. One at a time, after the name is approved. ★ Why: names become official, and the community still decides each one.
C. Post none. Everything stays here, but there are no official names.

3How exact should the locations be?

A. Exact GPS points. Most useful to scientists, but shows exact spots.
B. Place names only, like Itivia or Puirsuk (1st Lake). ★ Why: uses Inuktitut names, shows nothing new, and still says sea or lake.
C. "Chesterfield Inlet" only. Shows the least, but loses sea versus lake.

4Who gets credit for each name?

A. Only the person who suggested it. Honours them, but leaves out everyone who helped.
B. Only "the people of Igluligaarjuk". Shared by all, but nobody can have their own name on it.
C. Both. ★ Why: the community is always named, and each person can be named too, if they want.

5What happens to the water samples and DNA?

A. Kept frozen at the university, owned by the community. ★ Why: they stay usable for the community's future questions. Any new use needs permission, and the community can ask for them to be destroyed.
B. Sent back to Igluligaarjuk. Here in town, but DNA spoils without a very cold freezer (−80 °C).
C. Destroyed when the project ends. Nobody can misuse them, but no future studies either.

6Where do we keep the community's copy of the results?

A. Paper reports at the HTO and the school. Easy to read, but can't hold the full data.
B. Online only. Holds everything, but hard with slow internet.
C. Both: paper here, full data online. ★ Why: everyone can read the results, and nothing is lost.

Your choices

Taking part is your choice. You can say yes to some things and no to others. You will sign a consent form at the workshop; this page explains the choices on it.

🙋Taking partYou can join in, listen only, or leave at any time. No reason needed.
📷PhotosYou can say yes or no to being in photos.
🎙️RecordingsYou can say yes or no to being recorded (sound or video).
🏷️Your name on a microbe nameIf you suggest a name, you choose: your own name is listed, or only "the people of Igluligaarjuk".
💬Your wordsYou can say yes or no to being quoted in a report or paper.
📖Stories and knowledge you shareThey stay yours. We only use them in the ways you agree to.
👪YouthYoung people need a parent or guardian to agree as well.
Changing your mind. You can change your mind about a microbe name until it is published. After a name is published, we can't take it back. Later, scientists may move the microbe to a new group and give it a new name, but the record of your name stays.
Clear answers. We will always ask and wait for a clear answer. Silence is not taken as a yes.

Who to talk to

Ask the workshop leaders or the interpreter at any time. You can also talk to the HTO. They will help decide how the community's data is looked after.
