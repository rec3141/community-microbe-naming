# Nunavut practice: ethical, community-governed natural-science research in Inuit Nunangat (Kivalliq / Igluligaarjuk focus)

Compiled 2026-10-29. Every claim has an inline source. Where I could reach only search snippets or secondary summaries, and not the primary page, the claim is marked **[snippet-level]**. Organization-level information only; no personal contacts are recorded. The phone numbers seen in directories were deliberately left out.

## Q1. Local and regional organizations relevant to research in Chesterfield Inlet / Kivalliq, and what each does

### Takeaway
For an NRI-licensed project, the Nunavut Research Institute (NRI) itself sends the application to the Hamlet (municipal council), the HTO (Aqigiq Hunters and Trappers Organization), the District Education Authority (DEA), the Regional Inuit Association (Kivalliq Inuit Association) and NTI. Each can recommend approval, rejection or licence conditions. Direct engagement before and during that review is expected, and letters of support speed things up.

### Cited Findings
- **NRI review list.** The NRI says the agencies "most often consulted" are: Municipal Councils; Hunters and Trappers Organizations (HTOs); District Education Authorities (DEAs); Regional Inuit Associations (RIAs); Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated (NTI); Institutions of Public Government (IPGs); Government of Nunavut departments; and community interest groups such as community research committees. Consulted agencies get the full application plus a "reviewer recommendation form". They "may recommend approval or rejection" and "may also recommend terms and conditions for your license." — [NRI, *Obtaining a Research License under Nunavut's Scientists Act: A Guide for Applicants*, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Community veto in practice.** In a multi-community project, "You will receive a license to proceed with research activities only in the communities that have expressed support for the project." — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Aqigiq HTO (name verified).** It appears as "Aqigiq Hunters and Trappers Organization" on Legislative Assembly correspondence ([Assembly doc 4407](https://assembly.nu.ca/node/4407)). The community website spells it both "Aqigiq Hunters Trappers Association" and, in the URL slug, "aqigic" ([chesterfield-inlet.ca](https://chesterfield-inlet.ca/businesses/aqigic-hunters-and-trappers-organization/)). Its listed scope covers wildlife management and research, marine conservation and harvesting, environmental monitoring, safety training, and Kivalliq Wildlife Board funds. The community site sends wildlife and marine-mammal questions to the HTO. **[snippet-level]**
- **HTO as research partner elsewhere.** Analogous HTOs in Nunavut have co-led genomics: the Ekaluktutiak HTO (Cambridge Bay) and Kugluktuk Angoniatit Association co-led the MAGIC muskox genomics project. — [MAGIC report summary 2025](https://pubs.aina.ucalgary.ca/aina/MAGIC_report_summary_2025.pdf) **[snippet-level]**
- **Kivalliq Wildlife Board (KWB).** Its members are the chairs of each Kivalliq community HTO, with a separately elected chair. — [NIWS (Nunavut Inuit Wildlife Secretariat)](https://www.niws.ca/?p=6) **[snippet-level]** The KWB is the Regional Wildlife Organization (RWO), and RWOs can apply to the NWMB Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit Research Fund. — [NWMB IQRF page](https://nwmb.com/en/funding/inuit-qaujimajatuqangit-research-fund)
- **Kivalliq Inuit Association (KIA).** KIA is the RIA consulted by NRI. Separately, any project needing access to Inuit Owned Lands must get RIA permission; in Kivalliq the instrument is listed as a "Certificate of Exemption (to Access Inuit Owned Lands)". — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf); [NRI, Research Authorizations Nunavut](https://nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/Research_Authorizations_Nunavut.pdf) **[snippet-level for the KIA instrument name]**
- **NTI.** NTI is on NRI's standard consultee list ([NRI Guide](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)) and has a record of public intervention against research it considers disrespectful (see Q6).
- **Victor Sammurtok School (name verified).** K–12, roughly 100 students and about 22 staff according to a 2026 posting, with land/cultural programs. The building descends from the first Kivalliq school (Sir Joseph Bernier Federal Day School) and was renamed for a respected Elder. — [chesterfield-inlet.ca school page](https://chesterfield-inlet.ca/?p=2636); [Chesterfield DEA correspondence, Legislative Assembly](https://assembly.nu.ca/sites/default/files/TD-83-5(2)-EN-Correspondence-from-Chesterfield-DEA-to-the-Minister-of-Education.pdf) **[snippet-level]** Chronic staffing shortages have cancelled classes, which matters when scheduling school involvement. — [CBC via Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/classes-chesterfield-inlet-school-cancelled-170138394.html) **[snippet-level]**
- **DEA.** The Chesterfield Inlet District Education Authority writes directly to the Minister ([TD-83-5(2)](https://assembly.nu.ca/sites/default/files/TD-83-5(2)-EN-Correspondence-from-Chesterfield-DEA-to-the-Minister-of-Education.pdf)). DEAs are on NRI's consultee list, so school-based activity will normally go past the DEA.
- **Prior school–science links in Chesterfield Inlet.** University of Manitoba sea-ice researchers did fieldwork with Victor Sammurtok School students ("Chester teens drill for science"). — [Nunavut News](https://nnsl.com/nunavut-news/chester-teens-drill-for-science/); [UManitoba News](https://news.umanitoba.ca/?p=91124) **[snippet-level]**
- **Nunavut Research Institute / Nunavut Arctic College.** NRI is the science division of Nunavut Arctic College and issues Scientists Act licences. It has a facility in Rankin Inlet (Kivalliq), and the college's Kivalliq Campus is there too. Since 2023 the Rankin Inlet NAC lab has offered trichinella testing for Kivalliq communities. — [Wikipedia, Nunavut Arctic College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut_Arctic_College) **[secondary, snippet-level]**; [UArctic news, Jul 2026](https://www.uarctic.org/news/2026/7/learning-from-the-land-nunavut-arctic-college-and-inuit-led-research/). One college page reportedly uses the name "Nunavut Innovation & Research Institute"; the official name could not be confirmed (see Gaps).
- **Inuit Heritage Trust (IHT).** IHT documents traditional place names with Elders. Before entering a community it contacts the hamlet office and asks whether Elders are willing to share. — [Nunatsiaq News, "Inuit Heritage Trust maps traditional names before they're lost"](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/inuit-heritage-trust-maps-traditional-names-before-theyre-lost/); [IHT traditional place names](https://www.ihti.ca/traditional-place-names) **[snippet-level]**
- **Prior community-based research in Igluligaarjuk.**
  - A 2024 University of Manitoba MSc thesis interviewed 15 Inuit hunters, Elders and knowledge holders and ran two workshops on ecological change, with the community framing its own observations. — ["Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit in community-based monitoring of ecological changes in Igluligaarjuk (Chesterfield Inlet)", MSpace](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/items/3f871e00-3a28-4d3f-9013-fe78e83adfcd)
  - A Robin Rigby Trust-funded community wildlife-monitoring project runs on-the-land trips with youth, hunters and Elders. — [Community Conservation Research Network](https://www.communityconservation.net/people/sarah-newell/) **[snippet-level]**
- **Burden on reviewers.** The first survey of Nunavut research-licence-review organizations found:
  - they get up to 64 applications per year;
  - only 35% of reviewers are paid for their time;
  - only 10% give feedback on all the applications they review.

  Reviewers asked for more direct engagement with researchers and better access to results and reports. — [Beltrano et al. 2025, *Arctic* 78(3), doi:10.14430/arctic83527](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/arctic/article/view/83527)

### Inferences
- In a hamlet of this size, the Hamlet council, Aqigiq HTO and DEA/school are the practical gatekeepers, and the NRI review mostly lands on unpaid volunteers. Visiting these bodies in person, presenting in Inuktitut, and offering to pay for meeting time directly answers what reviewers asked for in Beltrano et al. 2025.
- The microbe-naming workshop touches several mandates at once:
  - marine and freshwater samples (HTO, KWB);
  - Inuktitut words (Elders; the language authority, see Q4);
  - youth and school (DEA);
  - Inuit-owned genetic data (KIA, NTI).

  Letters of support from the Hamlet, HTO and DEA are the minimum sensible set.

## Q2. Practical guides by Inuit and Northern organizations

### Takeaway
The core guidance stack is:
1. ITK/NRI *Negotiating Research Relationships with Inuit Communities: A Guide for Researchers* (2007).
2. ITK's *National Inuit Strategy on Research* (NISR, 2018).
3. NRI's licensing guide (latest May 2025), which writes NISR and TCPS2 Ch. 9 into licence expectations.
4. ICC's *Circumpolar Inuit Protocols for Equitable and Ethical Engagement* (June 2022).
5. Youth-authored ScIQ recommendations (Pedersen et al. 2020).

### Cited Findings
- **ITK & NRI (2007), *Negotiating Research Relationships with Inuit Communities: A Guide for Researchers*.**
  - Edited by Scot Nickels, Jamal Shirley and Gita Laidler, and published by Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami and the Nunavut Research Institute.
  - It follows the 1998 NRI/ITK community-facing booklet *Negotiating Research Relationships: A Guide for Communities*.
  - It covers understanding community concerns, choosing a level of community involvement, and initiating the relationship.
  - Some catalogues date it 2006; page counts differ between records.
  - Sources: [ITK page](https://itk.ca/negotiating-research-relationships-communities/); [OceanBestPractices record](https://repository.oceanbestpractices.org/handle/11329/1605); [mirror PDF, Uni Hamburg](https://epub.sub.uni-hamburg.de/epub/volltexte/2011/6959/pdf/06_068_ITK_NRR_booklet.pdf). The PDF returned 403, so I did not read the full text.
- **NRI licence guide (May 2025): key operating rules.**
  - **Review time.** External consultation takes at least 45 business days.
  - **Translated documents up front.** Review cannot start until NRI has the "translated project summary, institutional ethics approval, and translated consent forms".
  - **Fieldwork can change.** Researchers "may be required to modify your research project—such as adjusting methods, locations, or the timing of fieldwork" in response to consultation.
  - **Who is subject.** The Scientists Act covers research "involving people, air, land, or water", including research done remotely. Hydrology, limnology, water quality, oceanography and sea ice are listed licensed topics.
  - **Exemptions.** These are exempt from licensing: "consultations, engagement and outreach with community members to establish research partnerships… prior to the actual design of the research"; "visits to communities to disseminate research results"; and high-school projects. Licensing is "usually required" if academic partners will publish from the information gathered.
  - **Review criteria** include "the extent of local involvement proposed… including provisions for local training, employment, compensation, capacity building" and the quality of plans to return results.
  - **Annual reporting.** An Annual Summary Report is due before Dec 31, and it must be *translated*. Researchers must also send NRI final publications and theses.
  - **Results back to the community.** Researchers are "expected to share your research results directly with Nunavut agencies and community members" and should plan return visits and "involve community members in reviewing, interpreting, and validating your findings."
  - **Sample use is limited to the licence.** "Unless authorized under your license, you may not share research samples or specimens… outside the scope of your original licensed project. A license amendment or new research license must be obtained for any secondary research or analysis of archived samples." No "opportunistic" extra sampling is allowed.
  - **Filming.** Video of wildlife or people needs prior notice to the Nunavut Film Development Corporation.
  - **Other approvals.** Land- and water-based research also needs Nunavut Planning Commission review, NIRB screening, a Nunavut Water Board authorization to collect water samples, and RIA permission for Inuit Owned Lands. Where invertebrates or fish are sampled, a DFO licence to fish for scientific purposes is also required.
  - **Paying for engagement.** "It is considered best practice to compensate individuals or groups who participate in engagement activities."
  - Source for all of the above: [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **ITK National Inuit Strategy on Research (2018).**
  - It has five priority areas. Priority 4 is "access, ownership, and control over data and information".
  - Its objectives include Inuit self-determination in collecting, verifying, analyzing and disseminating Inuit-specific data, ownership of Inuit data by Inuit-appointed entities, and use of Inuktut in data platforms.
  - It is non-binding; one report notes it has "no teeth or enforcement mechanisms".
  - Sources: [ITK NISR Implementation Plan](https://itk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/ITK_NISR_Implementation-Plan_Electronic-Version.pdf); [Nunatsiaq, "five things you need to know"](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674the_national_inuit_research_strategy_five_things_you_need_to_know/); [uOttawa summary](https://www.uottawa.ca/library/sites/g/files/bhrskd381/files/2022-12/tscassa_legalandtechnologicalframeworks_28032019.pdf) **[snippet-level for objective wording]**
- **ICC Circumpolar Inuit Protocols for Equitable and Ethical Engagement (released June 2022).**
  - Inuit from Alaska, Canada, Greenland and Chukotka developed them over three years. They apply to research and to decision-making.
  - The eight protocols, as listed by secondary coverage (exact titles not confirmed against the original):
    1. "Nothing about us without us – always engage with Inuit"
    2. Recognize Indigenous Knowledge in its own right
    3. Practice good governance
    4. Communication with intent
    5. Exercising accountability – building trust
    6. Building meaningful partnerships
    7. Information, data sharing, ownership and permissions
    8. Equitably fund Inuit representation and knowledge
  - The document says to agree on appropriate compensation before work begins.
  - Sources: [ICC release](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/news/inuit-circumpolar-council-establishes-new-standard-in-international-engagement-with-the-release-of-circumpolar-inuit-protocols-for-equitable-and-ethical-engagement/); [Climate Policy Lab summary](https://www.climatepolicylab.org/communityvoices/2023/11/29/indigenous-engagement-what-we-can-learn-from-the-inuit) **[snippet-level]**
  - Precursor: the *ICC Ethical and Equitable Engagement Synthesis Report* (2021). Then-Chair Dalee Sambo Dorough said that "for decades, in the name of science, actions have been taken that ultimately disrespect or exploit Indigenous knowledge or take it out of context." — [ICC EEE Synthesis Report PDF](https://lcipp.unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2021-10/ICC-EEE-Synthesis-report.pdf); [RCI Eye on the Arctic, Sep 2021](https://www.rcinet.ca/eye-on-the-arctic/2021/09/15/itnl-inuit-org-approves-report-on-ethical-engagement-with-indigenous-knowledge/)
- **Pedersen et al. 2020, "ScIQ: an invitation and recommendations to combine science and Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit", *Arctic Science* 6:326–339, doi:10.1139/as-2020-0015.**
  - Inuit youth from four Nunavut communities developed it at a five-day summit in Cambridge Bay, working with Ikaarvik, a youth-led program based in Pond Inlet.
  - It contains 45 recommendations for before, during and after fieldwork.
  - It argues that incorporating IQ is "as much about how research is conducted as about the data collected."
  - Sources: [OceanBestPractices](https://repository.oceanbestpractices.org/handle/11329/2121); [openpolar](https://openpolar.no/Record/crcansciencepubl:10.1139%2Fas-2020-0015) **[snippet-level; full 45 recommendations not read]**
- **Related youth paper.** Barriers to Inuit youth engagement in environmental research include a lack of credentials and support systems. — [FACETS 2021, doi:10.1139/facets-2021-0035](https://facetsjournal.com/doi/10.1139/facets-2021-0035)
- **NWMB.** It has no standalone researcher-conduct guide. Its research guidance is expressed through funding:
  - the Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit Research Fund supports HTO/RWO/community-led IQ research, with calls in January and May;
  - the Nunavut Wildlife Studies Fund;
  - the Nunavut Wildlife Research Trust;
  - all assessed against regional priorities, including Kivalliq priorities for 2026–2029.
  - Sources: [NWMB funding priorities](https://nwmb.com/en/funding/priorities); [IQRF Funding Guide 2025](https://www.nwmb.com/images/stories/IQRF/IQRF_Funding_Guide_2025_ENG.pdf) **[snippet-level]**

### Inferences
- For a microbial genomics project, the clauses that bite are NRI's limits on secondary use of samples and the requirement for translated summaries and reports. Re-analysing 2025 samples or genomes for purposes outside the licence would need an amendment.
- A workshop held to return results may itself be licence-exempt ("visits to communities to disseminate research results"). However, gathering Inuktitut naming knowledge for publication arguably makes it social-science or IQ research, which would need NRI licensing. Confirm with NRI's Research Liaison office.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of the 2007 ITK/NRI guide, the ICC 2022 protocols PDF, or Pedersen et al. 2020, so the exact checklists are not reproduced here.
- I did not research Qikiqtani Inuit Association or Kitikmeot comparators, or the ArcticNet, Polar Knowledge Canada and Amundsen Science engagement guidelines, because of the tool-call budget.
- I did not find ITK's newer statements, such as an "Inuit approaches to research" document.

## Q3. What Inuit organizations and communities say they want from researchers

### Takeaway
The recurring asks are:
- Inuktitut-language communication;
- engagement before the proposal is fixed;
- local hiring and youth training;
- paid knowledge holders, with rates agreed in advance;
- in-person return of results with community interpretation;
- respect for harvest seasons and timing.

The Government of Nunavut's own honorarium benchmark for Elders and IQ knowledge holders is **$650 per full day / $325 per half day** (effective 2023-01-01).

### Cited Findings
- **GN benchmark (FAM Directive 810-1, effective Jan 1, 2023).**
  - Rates: IQ Knowledge Holder/Keeper, Elder, keynote speaker, chair or panellist $650 per full day and $325 per half day. Event attendee $400 per full day and $200 per half day. Ceremonial guest $350 per day.
  - The minimum payment is the half-day rate, and rates are reviewed every three years.
  - The directive defines an Elder as "a person not defined by chronological age, but through bestowments from their respective communities… Elders are identified by their community and will rarely identify themselves as such."
  - Honoraria may also cover "casual interpretation or translation services where the use of a personal service contract is not feasible."
  - Source: [GN FAM 810-1 Honoraria for Invited Event Participants](https://www.gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2022-12/fam_810-1_honoraria_for_invited_event_participants_to_be_posted_nov_24_2022.pdf). Read in full via text extraction; the next three-year review would fall around 2026, so check for an update.
- **Comparator.** The Northwest Territories government pays a fixed $250 per day honorarium to Indigenous knowledge holders. — [NWT Hansard, 2023](https://hansard.opennwt.ca/debates/2023/3/6/richard-edjericon-1/only) **[snippet-level]**
- **Paying Elders for IQ.** The Nunavut historical society argued that Elders should be paid for IQ, and called payment to Elders "an issue that Nunavut needs to look at across the territory". — [Nunatsiaq News](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674nunavut_elders_should_be_paid_for_tek_says_historical_society/) (date not shown) **[snippet-level]**
- **NRI on compensation and local involvement.** Its licence review weighs local training, employment and compensation. The application asks how residents will be "involved and compensated (e.g. pay rate for level of involvement)." — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf); [NRI application form 2024](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/240412%20license%20application%20-%20FILLABLE_2.pdf) **[snippet-level for form wording]**
- **Language and dissemination.** NRI requires translated summaries, consent forms and annual reports. It suggests dissemination by "newsletters, posters, fact sheets, radio interviews, social media, videos, podcasts, or in-person presentations", chosen to fit the audience's "age, background, language". — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Timing.** NRI's peak field season is May 1 to September 30. Consultation feedback can require changing "the timing of fieldwork". — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Reviewers want relationships, not paperwork.** Licence reviewers asked for "more direct engagement with researchers and better access to research outcomes and reports." — [Beltrano et al. 2025, *Arctic*](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/arctic/article/view/83527)
- **Compensation agreed first.** ICC protocols say to agree on compensation before work begins and to fund Inuit representation equitably. — [Climate Policy Lab summary of ICC EEE](https://www.climatepolicylab.org/communityvoices/2023/11/29/indigenous-engagement-what-we-can-learn-from-the-inuit) **[snippet-level]**
- **Data and results rarely come back.** In Nunatsiavut, two-thirds of research projects approved from 2011 to 2021 did not return data to the community. — [Ortenzi et al. 2024/2025, *Ambio* 54(2), doi:10.1007/s13280-024-02077-6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11662125/)
- **Cancellation duty.** If a project is cancelled, researchers must immediately notify local "translators, hotels, interpreters, outfitters, participants". — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Budget honoraria at no less than the GN rate ($650 per day for Elders, or $325 per half-day session). Pay youth and student participants an attendee-level rate or similar, and budget a professional interpreter separately.
- Avoid scheduling the workshop in peak harvesting periods, such as late summer and fall char runs, caribou and marine-mammal hunts, and spring goose season. Ask the HTO for local timing, because no source gave Chesterfield-specific harvest calendars.

### Gaps
- No Nunavut-wide research-specific Elder rate exists in the sources I found. The $650 figure is the GN rate for government events, used here as a benchmark.
- I found no source quantifying current interpreter rates in Kivalliq.
- I found no Chesterfield Inlet-specific statement of research expectations, for example from Hamlet minutes.

## Q4. Inuit place names, words and terminology approval (relevance to naming bacteria)

### Takeaway
Place names have a formal community-to-Minister pathway (IHT and GN Toponymy, with Elders consulted). New *terms* have a statutory standardization body, Inuit Uqausinginnik Taiguusiliuqtiit (IUT), whose board approves recommended terminology after consulting Elders and language experts. Neither has jurisdiction over Latin binomials under the SeqCode. They are, however, the legitimate Inuit authorities for spellings and for the meanings of Inuktut root words.

### Cited Findings
- **IUT's mandate and approved terms.** IUT is an independent statutory body under Nunavut's *Inuit Language Protection Act*, mandated to develop standardized terminology. Staff research usage and consult Elders and language experts through workshops and online forums; the Board of Directors approves terms. More than 600 recommended terms have been approved; "Inuktut" was adopted in 2015 as the term for Inuit languages, and "surrarnaqtuq" for cannabis. — [IUT Annual Report 2013–14 (Legislative Assembly)](https://assembly.nu.ca/sites/default/files/TD%209-4%283%29%20ENG%20Inuit%20Uqausinginnik%20Taiguusiliuqtiit%20Annual%20Report%202013-2014.pdf); [GN terminologist job description 2024](https://gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2024-01/08-12364%20-%20Terminologist%20Researcher.pdf) **[snippet-level]**
- **Limits and criticism.** IUT has the power to direct departments to adopt standard terms but has not used it. Critics object to standardization driven by the GN, and one MLA disliked the cannabis term. — [Nunatsiaq, "One Inuit language, many Inuit dialects"](https://nunatsiaq.com/?p=36136); [Nunatsiaq on IUT hearings](https://nunatsiaq.com/?p=21116) **[snippet-level]**
- **Place-name approval.** The GN Toponymy Program "works closely with communities to identify, document and give approval to local geographic names… consulting with Elders". Approved names go to the Minister of Culture and Heritage and then to federal staff for official maps. — [GN Toponymy Program](https://www.gov.nu.ca/en/department-culture-and-heritage/toponymy-program); [Nunatsiaq on IHT](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/inuit-heritage-trust-maps-traditional-names-before-theyre-lost/) **[snippet-level]**
- **Approval backlog (unverified).** Reports say the GN had not approved IHT-submitted names since 2012 and had had no toponymist since 2019. — [Nunatsiaq / ArcticToday on IHT](https://www.arctictoday.com/?p=56956) **[snippet-level; date and current status unverified]**
- **Name changes are possible.** In 2024 the GN moved to replace 15 place names containing slurs. — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/north/nunavut-govt-replace-15-place-names-slurs-1.7336721) **[snippet-level]**
- **Dialect.** Aivilingmiutut (Aivilik) is the western Hudson Bay dialect of Naujaat, Igluligaarjuk, Kangiqłiniq (Rankin Inlet) and Salliit (Coral Harbour). Tusaalanga notes that "there is no standard dialect in Canada". One Tusaalanga table lists "Igluligaarjuk" as a separate entry from Aivilingmiutut, so local speech may differ. — [Tusaalanga dialect page](https://tusaalanga.ca/node/2515); [Wikipedia, Aivilingmiutut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aivilingmiutut) **[secondary]**
- **Community name.** Igluligaarjuk is glossed as "place with few houses". — [GN community page](https://gov.nu.ca/communities/chesterfield-inlet) **[snippet-level]**
- **Reference dictionary.** *Inuktitut: A Multi-Dialectical Outline Dictionary (With an Aivilingmiutaq Base)* (Schneider/Spalding lineage) is a reference with an Aivilik base. — [UBC knowledgebase](https://knowledgebase.arts.ubc.ca/?p=2717) **[snippet-level; bibliographic details unverified]**

### Inferences
- The legitimate model for bacterial names is the IHT/Toponymy one: record the Elder-endorsed form, its syllabics, the Roman orthography and its meaning, and record community approval.
- Consider sending the final Inuktut roots or terms to IUT for information or comment. IUT approval is not legally required for a scientific name, but it signals respect, and IUT may want the new microbiology words (for example "bacteria") for its term bank.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether IUT has an existing Inuktut term for "bacteria/microbe", or how a community or individual submits a term to IUT.
- I did not confirm IHT's current place-name holdings for the Chesterfield Inlet area.
- Inuktut Tusaalanga's Aivilingmiutut coverage (e.g., audio lessons) was not assessed in detail.

## Q5. Examples of community-led or co-led environmental science and genomics with good data governance

### Takeaway
The best-documented models are:
- SIKU (Arctic Eider Society, Sanikiluaq): an Inuit-built platform where "you own your own data", which is not open data.
- The MAGIC muskox genomics project, co-led by Nunavut HTOs and using Local Contexts labels.
- The Nunatsiavut Government Research Advisory Committee and Research Centre, which audited data return.
- Local Contexts Biocultural (BC) Labels, the machine-readable tool most directly applicable to microbial genome deposits.

### Cited Findings
- **SIKU.**
  - Built by the Arctic Eider Society, an Inuit-led charity in Sanikiluaq, Nunavut. Users retain ownership ("You own your own data") and control privacy and sharing. The platform is explicitly not open-data, and sharing content does not license SIKU to use it otherwise. AES says it develops SIKU but never owns user data. Over 100 active projects.
  - Polar Knowledge Canada describes SIKU as built on "a framework of self-determination and data sovereignty".
  - Sources: [SIKU Guiding Principles / Terms](https://about.siku.org/terms); [SIKU Indigenous Data Sovereignty page](https://siku.org/?p=3902); [2024–2025 SIKU report](https://about.siku.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2024-2025-SIKU-REPORT.pdf); [Polar Knowledge Canada, Jun 2025](https://www.canada.ca/en/polar-knowledge/news/2025/06/a-milestone-in-realizing-a-vision-for-canadian-leadership-in-arctic-science-new-exhibition-about-indigenous-knowledge-app-opens-at-canadian-high-ar.html) **[mostly self-description; no independent audit found]**
- **MAGIC (Muskox and Genomics in the Community).** Co-led by the Ekaluktutiak HTO (Cambridge Bay), the Kugluktuk Angoniatit Association and the Olokhaktomiut HTC (Ulukhaktok, NWT). The team is developing metadata labels with Local Contexts so communities can assert data rights. — [MAGIC report summary 2025](https://pubs.aina.ucalgary.ca/aina/MAGIC_report_summary_2025.pdf); [Athabasca Univ. record](https://pure.athabascau.ca/en/publications/muskox-ovibos-moschatus-and-genomics-in-the-community-magic-setti/) **[snippet-level]**
- **Local Contexts BC/TK Labels.**
  - BC Labels extend TK Labels to genetic resources and genomic data. Only an Indigenous community may apply them, and they are human- and machine-readable.
  - The European Reference Genome Atlas has guidance for adding them to genomic metadata.
  - They express community expectations but do not themselves create legal rights.
  - Sources: [Local Contexts BC Labels](https://localcontexts.org/labels/biocultural-labels/); [ERGA guidance](https://zenodo.org/records/8088227/files/Guidance%20Documentation%20for%20Implementing%20the%20Traditional%20Knowledge%20and%20Biocultural%20Labels%20in%20the%20European%20Reference%20Genome%20Atlas.pdf); [ENRICH policy brief 2024](https://zenodo.org/records/13172148/files/ENRICH%20Policy%20Brief%20%202024.pdf)
- **Nunatsiavut.** The Nunatsiavut Government Research Advisory Committee approves research. Its audit of 2011–2021 projects found two-thirds never returned data. The authors give recommendations for researchers, funders and Indigenous research boards. — [Ortenzi, Flowers, Pamak, Saunders, Schmidt & Bailey, *Ambio* 2025, 54(2), doi:10.1007/s13280-024-02077-6 (OA)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11662125/)
- **Paulatuk (Inuvialuit).** The HTO co-developed a data-governance "Statement of Shared Understanding" for Traditional Knowledge documentation. — [Frontiers in Environmental Science 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2025.1521494/pdf) **[snippet-level; attribution to this exact paper unverified]**
- **Inuit Nunangat Research Program.** ITK-administered Inuit-led funding, including an Arctic char health project whose results go to the Kugluktuk HTO. — [ITK INRP recipients](https://itk.ca/inuit-nunangat-research-program-funding-recipients/) **[snippet-level]**
- **Genome Canada "Fish Finders".** Arctic char genomics in Gjoa Haven; the HTO's role and data terms were not found. — [Genome Canada](https://genomecanada.ca/fish-finders/) **[snippet-level]**

### Inferences
- A practical template for this project combines:
  - a short data-sharing agreement or MOU with the Hamlet, HTO and/or KIA covering samples, genomes and names;
  - Local Contexts BC Notices or Labels on the NCBI/ENA BioProject and SeqCode registry entries;
  - a copy of plain-language results, and possibly the data, held on an Inuit-controlled platform such as SIKU or with the HTO.

### Gaps
- I did not find a Nunavut environmental eDNA or microbial-genomics project with a published data agreement.
- SmartICE governance, Kivalliq water-monitoring programs and the Inuvialuit research model were not researched in detail because of the call budget.

## Q6. Negative examples and lessons

### Takeaway
The documented Inuit-specific harms are mostly about wildlife-handling research, as in NTI's 2007 demand to halt "intrusive" research, and about data and results not being returned, as with the 2007–08 Inuit Health Survey and the Nunatsiavut audit. Classic DNA-misuse cases (Nuu-chah-nulth, Havasupai) are First Nations / Native American cases and should be presented as such. I could not verify a specific Inuit DNA-sample controversy.

### Cited Findings
- **NTI, December 5, 2007.** At its AGM in Rankin Inlet, NTI demanded a halt to intrusive wildlife research. It cited:
  - 2,364 polar bears darted or handled in Davis Strait over three years, against an estimated population of about 2,100;
  - 20 female caribou collared on calving grounds near Baker Lake, with about eight dying soon after;
  - six belugas drowned during tagging.

  NTI said: "Our requests have largely been ignored, and this is unacceptable… It is disrespectful of animals and Inuit." — [NTI news release](https://www.tunngavik.com/news/nti-demands-intrusive-scientific-wildlife-research-be-halted/)
- **2007–08 Inuit Health Survey (Qanuippitali).** Samples of bodily fluids and survey data were collected from 1,923 Nunavut Inuit, including in Kivalliq communities. NTI criticized inadequate reporting of results, and a Nunatsiaq column called it "IPY's biggest dud". — [Nunatsiaq, "NTI slams handling of 2007-08 Inuit health survey"](https://nunatsiaq.com/?p=22014); [Nunatsiaq, "Qanuippitali: IPY's biggest dud"](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674qanuippitali_ipys_biggest_dud/) **[snippet-level]**
- **Nuu-chah-nulth (First Nations, BC).**
  - 883 blood samples were collected from 1982 to 1985 for arthritis research. When no genetic link was found, they were used for unconsented population-genetics, migration and HIV research and shared widely.
  - Samples were returned to UBC around 2013. UBC and Utah adopted re-consent policies, and the Nation formed its own research ethics committee.
  - Sources: [Ha-Shilth-Sa, "Blood taken for research destroyed"](https://hashilthsa.com/node/12020); [Wiwchar 2004 (PDF)](https://www.igb.illinois.edu/sites/default/files/Wiwchar%202004%20Nuu-chah-nulth.pdf); [EHP 2001 archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080923142050/http://www.ehponline.org/docs/2001/109-5/spheres.html) **[snippet-level]**
- **Havasupai (US).** Members said they consented only to diabetes research, but their samples were used more broadly; the case was litigated. — [Stanford Law, "DNA Returned To Tribe, Raising Questions About Consent"](https://law.stanford.edu/?p=15921) **[snippet-level]**
- **Return as repair.** In 2019, more than 200 Galiwin'ku (Aboriginal Australian) blood samples from 1968–69 were returned after 11 months of consultation. — [ANU, "Bloodlines and reconciliation"](https://www.anu.edu.au/news/all-news/bloodlines-and-reconciliation) **[snippet-level]**
- **Data return gap.** Two-thirds of Nunatsiavut-approved projects did not return data. — [Ortenzi et al., *Ambio*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11662125/)

### Inferences
- For environmental microbes the risk is less about human identity and more about these points:
  - secondary use of samples or genomes beyond what was agreed, which NRI explicitly prohibits without amendment;
  - names and meanings being detached from the community;
  - commercialization (bioprospecting) without benefit-sharing;
  - results never coming back.

  These map directly onto the Nuu-chah-nulth lesson (scope creep) and the Nunatsiavut lesson (non-return).

### Gaps
- **Unverified:** a specific Greenland or Canadian Inuit DNA-sample controversy, for example 1960s–70s International Biological Programme samples from Igloolik, or Greenlandic biobank disputes. Searches returned nothing Inuit-specific, so do not cite one without further checking.
- I found no Nunatsiaq article on "helicopter research" complaints specific to Kivalliq or Chesterfield Inlet.

## Q7. Practical recommendations for consent at the workshop (group consent, photos, recording, attribution)

### Takeaway
Use layered consent:
- community-level agreement in writing (Hamlet, HTO and DEA letters, ideally with an MOU on names and data);
- individual, translated, opt-in consent for photos, audio and video, and for being named;
- parental consent for minors via the school/DEA.

Default to attributing names to the community, for example "proposed by Igluligaarjuk community members, Aivilingmiutut", and name individuals only where they opt in.

### Cited Findings
- **Translated consent.** NRI requires translated consent forms as part of the licence package. — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Video.** Video of people or wildlife requires prior notice to the Nunavut Film Development Corporation. — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Confidentiality.** Results communication "must respect and protect the anonymity and confidentiality of research participants as outlined in the informed consent agreements." — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Applicable ethics frameworks.** Licence holders must follow NISR and TCPS2 Chapter 9 (Research Involving First Nations, Inuit and Métis Peoples). — [NRI Guide, May 2025](https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/License%20Application%20Guidleines_May2025.pdf)
- **Who counts as an Elder.** Elders "are identified by their community and will rarely identify themselves as such." Ask the Hamlet or HTO to identify Elder participants rather than self-selecting. — [GN FAM 810-1](https://www.gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2022-12/fam_810-1_honoraria_for_invited_event_participants_to_be_posted_nov_24_2022.pdf)
- **Data permissions.** ICC protocol 7 ("Information, data sharing, ownership and permissions") and NISR Priority 4 support community control over how names and knowledge are used. — [ICC release](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/news/inuit-circumpolar-council-establishes-new-standard-in-international-engagement-with-the-release-of-circumpolar-inuit-protocols-for-equitable-and-ethical-engagement/); [ITK NISR](https://itk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/ITK_NISR_Implementation-Plan_Electronic-Version.pdf)
- **Machine-readable attribution.** Local Contexts BC Labels can record attribution and conditions in genomic metadata, and only the community can apply them. — [Local Contexts](https://localcontexts.org/labels/biocultural-labels/)

### Inferences (practical checklist; derived, not sourced as a single guide)
1. **Before the workshop:**
   - Confirm with NRI whether the workshop needs a licence or an amendment to the 2025 licence, since collecting Inuktitut naming knowledge for publication may count as IQ or social-science research.
   - Get Hamlet, HTO and DEA letters.
   - Agree on honoraria (GN benchmark $650 per day / $325 per half day) and on interpreter fees.
2. **Consent materials:** syllabics plus Roman orthography plus English, read aloud by the interpreter. Separate checkboxes for:
   - (a) participating;
   - (b) being photographed;
   - (c) audio or video recording;
   - (d) having their name credited on a bacterial name or in publications;
   - (e) quotes being used.
3. **Youth:** parental or guardian consent through Victor Sammurtok School and the DEA. Use no identifiable images of minors without explicit guardian opt-in.
4. **Attribution:**
   - Record the etymology, the dialect, and "proposed by the community of Igluligaarjuk" in the SeqCode protologue.
   - Credit named individuals, such as an Elder who proposed a root, only on opt-in.
   - Offer co-authorship or acknowledgement to the HTO, Hamlet or school.
5. **Withdrawal and veto:** allow the community to withdraw or change a proposed name before registration. Spell out that once a name is validly published under SeqCode it is hard to change.
6. **Return:** deliver a translated plain-language summary and posters, give radio (CBC Kivalliq / community radio) an update, and send the HTO and Hamlet the final register entries.

### Gaps
- I found no Inuit-authored guidance specifically on naming organisms (biological nomenclature) or on group versus individual attribution of names.
- I found no Nunavut source on group or collective consent procedures for workshops. The recommendations above extrapolate from NRI, ICC and TCPS2 principles.
