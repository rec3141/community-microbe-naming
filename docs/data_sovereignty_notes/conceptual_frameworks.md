## 1. The global IDSov movement: GIDA, CARE vs FAIR, national networks, Kukutai & Taylor 2016

### Takeaway
IDSov is the right of Indigenous Peoples to govern the collection, ownership and use of data about their peoples, lands, waters and resources. It grew from national networks (Australia, Aotearoa NZ, US, Canada) around 2015–2018, went global through GIDA, and its best-known product is the CARE Principles (released 2019, formally published 2020), designed to sit alongside the FAIR principles of open science rather than replace them.

### Cited Findings
**Kukutai & Taylor 2016 (foundational book)**
- Kukutai T, Taylor J (eds). 2016. *Indigenous Data Sovereignty: Toward an Agenda.* CAEPR Research Monograph No. 38, ANU Press, Canberra. DOI: 10.22459/CAEPR38.11.2016. Open access: https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n2140/pdf/book.pdf. It is the first edited volume to set out IDSov as a field, with chapters from Aotearoa, Australia, Canada and the US. It frames data "about" Indigenous peoples as a site of colonial power and argues for Indigenous rights over data under UNDRIP. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- Follow-up volume: Walter M, Kukutai T, Carroll SR, Rodriguez-Lonebear D (eds). 2021. *Indigenous Data Sovereignty and Policy.* Routledge. Open access. DOI: 10.4324/9780429273957. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**

**CARE Principles and GIDA**
- CARE stands for **C**ollective benefit, **A**uthority to control, **R**esponsibility, **E**thics. It was developed by the International Indigenous Data Sovereignty Interest Group (within the Research Data Alliance) in consultation with Indigenous Peoples, scholars, non-profits and governments. It is meant to complement data-centric principles such as FAIR by drawing attention to the people and purpose of data. — [CARE statement as reproduced in a UN Global Digital Compact submission](https://www.un.org/digital-emerging-technologies/sites/www.un.org.techenvoy/files/GDC-submission_WAMPUM_Lab_and_the_Collaboratory_for_Indigenous.pdf) **[verified this session, via search summary]**; GIDA's page: https://www.gida-global.org/care
- Formal citation: Carroll SR, Garba I, Figueroa-Rodríguez OL, Holbrook J, Lovett R, Materechera S, Parsons M, Raseroka K, Rodriguez-Lonebear D, Rowe R, Sara R, Walker JD, Anderson J, Hudson M. 2020. "The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance." *Data Science Journal* 19:43. DOI: 10.5334/dsj-2020-043. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- Timeline: drafted at an Indigenous data sovereignty workshop held with International Data Week in Gaborone, Botswana (Nov 2018); released by GIDA in Sept 2019; published in the 2020 *Data Science Journal* paper. GIDA formed in 2019 as the alliance of national IDSov networks: Te Mana Raraunga, Maiam nayri Wingara, USIDSN and Canadian/other partners. — https://www.gida-global.org **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- The CARE sub-principles in brief:
  - **Collective benefit:** inclusive development and innovation, improved governance and citizen engagement, equitable outcomes.
  - **Authority to control:** recognize rights and interests, data for governance, governance of data.
  - **Responsibility:** positive relationships, expanding capability and capacity, Indigenous languages and worldviews.
  - **Ethics:** minimizing harm and maximizing benefit, justice, future use.
  - Source: Carroll et al. 2020 above. **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
  - Note for this workshop: CARE R3 explicitly names "Indigenous languages and worldviews".
- FAIR stands for Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable. Citation: Wilkinson MD et al. 2016. "The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship." *Scientific Data* 3:160018. DOI: 10.1038/sdata.2016.18. FAIR is about the data. CARE is about the people and purpose. GIDA's slogan is "Be FAIR and CARE". — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- Operationalizing both: Carroll SR, Herczog E, Hudson M, Russell K, Stall S. 2021. "Operationalizing the CARE and FAIR Principles for Indigenous data futures." *Scientific Data* 8:108. DOI: 10.1038/s41597-021-00892-0. It proposes provenance, permissions and protocols as machine-actionable metadata, for example Local Contexts Labels and Notices attached to datasets in repositories. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- Extension to genomic benefit-sharing: Carroll SR, Garba I, Plevel R, et al. 2022. "Using Indigenous standards to implement the CARE principles: setting expectations through tribal research codes." *Frontiers in Genetics* 13:1052620. DOI: 10.3389/fgene.2022.1052620. A search snippet says the paper notes that research using environmental DNA must engage ethically with Indigenous communities. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fgene.2022.1052620) **[title wording UNVERIFIED; the search surfaced the PDF as "Extending the CARE Principles from tribal research policies to benefit sharing in genomic research"]**

**National networks**
- **Te Mana Raraunga (Māori Data Sovereignty Network), Aotearoa NZ.** Founded 2015–2016. It published a Charter and, in Oct 2018, the *Principles of Māori Data Sovereignty* (rangatiratanga/authority, whakapapa/relationships, whanaungatanga/obligations, kotahitanga/collective benefit, manaakitanga/reciprocity, kaitiakitanga/guardianship). It defines Māori data as including data "about Māori ... and the environments we have relationships with", which explicitly covers environmental data. — https://www.temanararaunga.maori.nz **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- **Maiam nayri Wingara Indigenous Data Sovereignty Collective, Australia.** Formed 2016. The 2018 Indigenous Data Sovereignty Summit Communiqué (Canberra, June 2018) asserts Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander rights to exercise control of the data ecosystem, data that is contextual and disaggregated, relevance, and accountability. — https://www.maiamnayriwingara.org **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- **US Indigenous Data Sovereignty Network (USIDSN).** Founded 2016, hosted by the Native Nations Institute, University of Arizona. Key output: Rainie SC, Rodriguez-Lonebear D, Martinez A. 2017. *Policy Brief: Indigenous Data Sovereignty in the United States.* — https://usindigenousdata.org ; [NNI data sovereignty keyword page](https://nnigovernance.cals.arizona.edu/keywords/data-sovereignty?page=1) **[network existence verified via search; details from prior knowledge]**
- **2024–2026 developments.** CARE is now being pushed beyond human data into environmental modelling. A 2026 *One Earth* paper, "Why environmental and ecological researchers who use models should CARE", argues that integrating CARE into scientific modelling is essential to uphold Indigenous rights. — [One Earth 2026](https://www.cell.com/one-earth/fulltext/S2590-3322(26)00214-9) **[existence verified via search; authors not checked]**. An *Annual Review of Genomics and Human Genetics* review, "Indigenous Data Sovereignty in Genomics and Human Genetics: Genomic Equity and Justice for Indigenous Peoples" (c. 2025), is a current synthesis. — [Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-genom-022024-125543) **[existence verified; authors/year UNVERIFIED]**. ASM (American Society for Microbiology) published "Indigenous Knowledge: Connecting Data, Land and Story" (Nov 2025), a sign that IDSov is now reaching mainstream microbiology. — [ASM 2025](https://asm.org/articles/2025/november/indigenous-knowledge-connecting-data,-land-and-sto) **[existence verified; content not read]**

### Inferences
- CARE is the most useful single framework to show both audiences. It is short, global and co-authored by Indigenous scholars, and its "R" explicitly includes Indigenous languages and worldviews. "FAIR and CARE" is a good slide framing for researchers who must deposit MAGs in open archives (INSDC/NCBI/ENA). The SeqCode also requires genomes to be publicly available.
- Te Mana Raraunga's definition of data includes environments Indigenous peoples have relationships with. That gives a precedent for treating microbial DNA from Inuit waters as "Indigenous data" even though it is non-human.

### Gaps
- I could not open gida-global.org or the *Data Science Journal* article this session to re-confirm the exact sub-principle wording. Check it before quoting it verbatim on slides.
- I did not find a GIDA statement specific to microbes or environmental DNA.

## 2. OCAP® (First Nations Information Governance Centre)

### Takeaway
OCAP® (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession) is a First Nations framework, trademarked by FNIGC. It is often loosely applied to "Indigenous" data in Canada, but Inuit organizations have their own frameworks (NISR, the ICC Protocols, and the forthcoming National Inuit Data Strategy). For Inuit contexts, cite those rather than OCAP®.

### Cited Findings
- OCAP® stands for Ownership, Control, Access and Possession. The principles were established in 1998 during the First Nations Regional Longitudinal Health Survey (RHS) process, by what became the First Nations Information Governance Centre (FNIGC, incorporated 2010). "OCAP® is a registered trademark of the First Nations Information Governance Centre." — https://fnigc.ca/ocap-training/ **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified; fnigc.ca returned HTTP 403 this session]**
- FNIGC's mandate is First Nations-specific. FNIGC states that OCAP® asserts First Nations' jurisdiction over their data. The trademark exists partly to stop misuse and dilution, including generic use for all Indigenous peoples. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- In a distinctions-based approach (First Nations, Inuit and Métis), Canada's UN Declaration Act Action Plan commits to "distinctions-based Indigenous data strategies" and to Indigenous-led data governance. — summarized in [CSPS, "Indigenous Data Sovereignty" (DDN3-A11)](https://www.csps-efpc.gc.ca/tools/articles/indigenous-data-sovereignty-eng.aspx) **[verified via search summary]**
- Inuit practice: ITK's NISR (2018) independently uses the phrase "access, ownership and control" for Inuit data (see section 3). ICC's Protocol 7 (2022) uses "access, ownership and control over information, data and materials". The concepts overlap with OCAP® but are Inuit-authored. — [ITK NISR](https://itk.ca/projects/national-inuit-strategy-on-research/); ICC 2022, see section 4 **[verified this session]**

### Inferences
- For the workshop: avoid presenting "OCAP" as the Inuit framework. If you mention it, name it as a First Nations framework (with ®) for comparison, then point to the Inuit-authored equivalents. OCAP® also centres on information about First Nations people and communities. Applying it to environmental microbial genomes would be a stretch even in a First Nations context.

### Gaps
- I could not re-open fnigc.ca (403) to quote its current wording on scope or trademark.
- I found no FNIGC or ITK statement that explicitly discusses OCAP® versus Inuit frameworks. The "why not Inuit" point is an inference from the distinctions-based approach and authorship.

## 3. Inuit-specific frameworks: ITK (NISR 2018, Inuit Nunangat Policy 2022, National Inuit Data Strategy), regional and Nunavut data work

### Takeaway
The anchor Inuit document is ITK's **National Inuit Strategy on Research (NISR, March 2018)**. Its Priority Area 4 is "Ensure Inuit access, ownership, and control over data and information", and it explicitly extends to data about wildlife and the environment, not just people. A **National Inuit Data Strategy** has been under development since federal Budget 2021 funding. As of the latest sources found (ICC Canada annual report 2025–26), it was **not yet published**. NTI is separately developing a **Nunavut Data Strategy**.

### Cited Findings
**NISR (2018)**
- Five priority areas: (1) Advance Inuit governance in research; (2) Enhance the ethical conduct of research; (3) Align funding with Inuit research priorities; (4) Ensure Inuit access, ownership, and control over data and information; (5) Build capacity in Inuit Nunangat research. — [ITK NISR report PDF](https://itk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/ITK_NISR-Report_English_low_res.pdf); [ITK NISR project page](https://itk.ca/projects/national-inuit-strategy-on-research/) **[priority 4 wording verified this session; the other four titles from prior knowledge and the search summary]**
- Scope includes the environment. ITK frames self-determination in research as "determining how data and information about our people, wildlife, and environment is collected, stored, used, and shared", and says research governance bodies, policies and practices "must be transformed to respect Inuit self-determination in Inuit Nunangat research." — [ITK NISR page](https://itk.ca/projects/national-inuit-strategy-on-research/) **[verified via search summary]**
- NISR Implementation Plan (Sept 2018) objectives include ensuring ownership of Inuit data by Inuit-appointed entities and investing in Inuit-led data infrastructure, with timelines to 2020–2022. These timelines are now dated. — [NISR Implementation Plan PDF](https://www.itk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/ITK_NISR_Implementation-Plan_Electronic-Version.pdf) **[verified via search summary]**
- NISR launch date: 22 March 2018. — [ITK launch release](https://itk.ca/inuit-tapiriit-kanatami-launches-the-national-inuit-strategy-on-research/?lang=iuq); [Nunatsiaq News, "five things you need to know"](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674the_national_inuit_research_strategy_five_things_you_need_to_know/) **[date from prior knowledge; sources found]**
- Older practical guide (flag: 2007): ITK & Nunavut Research Institute. 2007. *Negotiating Research Relationships with Inuit Communities: A Guide for Researchers.* — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**

**Inuit Nunangat Policy (2022)**
- The Inuit-Crown Partnership Committee endorsed the Inuit Nunangat Policy in April 2022. It commits federal departments to recognize Inuit Nunangat as a distinct geographic, cultural and political region and to design federal programs and policies (including research funding and programs) accordingly. — https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca (Inuit Nunangat Policy page) **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- 2026 status: the Government of Canada's 2026 UN Declaration Act annual report (Annex B, Inuit Priorities) lists "Co-develop guidance document to support the implementation of the Inuit Nunangat Policy" as "Underway" (Inuit Priority 1 and Priority 9 milestones). The same annex has **no** separate measure for Inuit data or data sovereignty. — [Justice Canada, 2026 report, Annex B](https://www.justice.gc.ca/eng/declaration/report-rapport/2026/b3.html) **[verified this session]**

**National Inuit Data Strategy (in progress)**

ICC Canada participated on this ITK-coordinated committee throughout the year, with substantial progress made at this in-person meeting toward a published national Inuit Data Strategy." — [ICC Canada 2025–2026 Annual Report](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026-AnnualReport-Digital-ENG-Final.pdf) **[verified this session; text extracted from PDF]**

Both are described as "upcoming" with no date. NTI's stated principles: Inuit control data management so communities can access data about them and from their communities; data handled in line with Inuit values; data "used for purposes and shared with parties only that Inuit agree with". NTI also says non-Inuit researchers "should be better educated on Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit". — [Arctic Focus, Nowosad, "Upholding Inuit data sovereignty in Nunavut" (undated; © 2026 page)](https://arcticfocus.org/stories/upholding-inuit-data-sovereignty-nunavut) **[verified this session]**

- Human-data exemplar: the Qanuippitaa? National Inuit Health Survey is Inuit-led and Inuit-determined. — [Arctic Focus](https://arcticfocus.org/stories/upholding-inuit-data-sovereignty-nunavut)

## 4. Inuit Circumpolar Council: EEE Protocols (2022) and ICC on genetic resources and biodiversity

### Takeaway
The ICC's *Circumpolar Inuit Protocols for Equitable and Ethical Engagement* (June 2022) is the most directly applicable Inuit-authored guidance for outside researchers. Protocol 7 claims Inuit "access, ownership and control over information, data and materials pertaining to our ... resources and homelands", including **scientific** raw data collected in Inuit Nunaat. That wording plausibly covers water samples and the DNA derived from them.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Inuit Circumpolar Council. 2022. *Circumpolar Inuit Protocols for Equitable and Ethical Engagement.* Developed over about three years from a synthesis of Inuit-produced guidelines and workshops with delegates from Alaska, Canada, Greenland and Chukotka. Approved by the ICC Executive Council and released June 2022. — [ICC press release](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/news/inuit-circumpolar-council-establishes-new-standard-in-international-engagement-with-the-release-of-circumpolar-inuit-protocols-for-equitable-and-ethical-engagement/); [PDF (UNFCCC LCIPP mirror)](https://lcipp.unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-06/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf); [ARCUS](https://www.arcus.org/arctic-info/archive/33236) **[verified this session]**
- The eight protocols, as listed in the table of contents: 1 'Nothing About Us Without Us' – Always Engage with Inuit; 2 Recognize Indigenous Knowledge in its Own Right; 3 Practice Good Governance; 4 Communicate with Intent; 5 Exercise Accountability – Building Trust; 6 Build Meaningful Partnerships; 7 Information and Data Sharing, Ownership, and Permissions; 8 Equitably Fund Inuit Representation and Knowledge. — [EEE Protocols PDF](https://lcipp.unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-06/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf) **[verified this session]**
- Protocol 1 directives (quoted): implement UNDRIP; involve Inuit from the beginning; "Approach Inuit engagement through 'Free, Prior, and Informed Consent' ... which includes the ability to say 'yes', 'yes with conditions', and 'no'. It is important to note that silence does not mean agreement or yes." — same PDF, p.17 **[verified this session]**
- Protocol 2 (quoted): "Indigenous Knowledge must not be translated, integrated into, or validated by science". Respecting "Inuit values, our worldviews, perspectives, languages and experiences" is required to understand IK. — same PDF, p.18 **[verified this session]**
- **Protocol 7 (quoted):** "Inuit need access, ownership and control over information, data and materials pertaining to our knowledge, people, culture, resources and homelands. This includes application to all aspects of monitoring and research design, information/data management planning and data collection." The directives include:
  - "Access to Information and Data – Inuit access to all raw information and data collected within Inuit Nunaat should be agreed upon and ensured. Including scientific information and data."
  - "Involve Inuit in the analysis and interpretation of results."
  - "Give credit where credit is due."
  - "Require Inuit review and agreement on all materials and products ... prior to public release."
  - "Report back in appropriate and accessible language."
  - FPIC applies to ownership and permission.
  - Source: same PDF, pp.26–28. **[verified this session]**
- The protocols' own copyright note models IDSov: "The Indigenous Knowledge ... in this report, remains the collective intellectual property of the communities, organizations and people who provided the information." — same PDF, p.4 **[verified this session]**
- ICC and the Convention on Biological Diversity (2025–2026): ICC Canada leads ICC engagement on the CBD, whose goals include "fair and equitable sharing of the benefits from the use of genetic resources". ICC participates through the International Indigenous Forum on Biodiversity. It sent a delegation to the first meeting of the new CBD **Subsidiary Body on Article 8(j)** (Panama City, Oct 2025). It advocates a distinctions-based approach and opposes conflating "Indigenous Peoples" with "local communities". In Dec 2025 it pushed for inclusion of Indigenous Knowledge in marine biodiversity monitoring. ICC Vice-Chair Herb Nakimayak (18 Nov 2025): "Parties must respect Indigenous governance and self-determination within the CBD and the Subsidiary Body on Article 8(j)." ICC anticipated COP17 in October 2026. — [ICC Canada 2025–2026 Annual Report](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026-AnnualReport-Digital-ENG-Final.pdf) **[verified this session]**
- Older ICC foundation document (flag: 2009): *A Circumpolar Inuit Declaration on Sovereignty in the Arctic*, cited in Protocol 1. — https://iccalaska.org/wp-icc/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Signed-Inuit-Sovereignty-Declaration-11x17.pdf **[cited within the EEE PDF; verified]**

### Inferences
- Protocol 7's "materials pertaining to our ... resources and homelands" and "all raw ... scientific information and data collected within Inuit Nunaat" is the clearest Inuit-authored text supporting the view that physical water and DNA samples, raw reads and MAGs from Igluligaarjuk waters fall under Inuit access and permission expectations.
- Protocol 7's "review ... prior to public release" sits in tension with SeqCode and INSDC requirements for public genome deposition. That is a concrete point to discuss openly at the workshop (see section 8).

### Gaps
- I did not find a specific ICC position paper on **digital sequence information (DSI)** or microbial genetic resources. The annual report discusses CBD and genetic resources in general only.
- COP17 (Oct 2026) outcomes are not covered. It was ongoing or just concluded at the time of writing and I did not search for results.

## 5. Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit (IQ) principles relevant to research

### Takeaway
The Government of Nunavut's IQ guiding principles (commonly listed as eight) give values-based language for describing ethical research partnerships in Inuit terms. Several map directly onto CARE and the ICC Protocols. Spellings and lists vary between sources, so use the Government of Nunavut's official list for slides.

### Cited Findings
- Commonly cited definitions:
  - **Pijitsirniq**: serving and providing for family and/or community. In governance, "a person with the power to make decisions must exercise that power to serve the people to whom he or she is responsible."
  - **Aajiiqatigiinniq**: decision-making through discussion and consensus.
  - **Piliriqatigiinniq / Ikajuqtigiinniq**: working together for a common cause, a collaborative relationship.
  - **Avatittinnik Kamatsiarniq**: respect and care for the land, animals and the environment.
  - Sources: [Conference Board of Canada, IQ Principles](https://www.conferenceboard.ca/in-fact/inuit-qaujimajatuqangit-principles); [GN, *Incorporating Inuit Societal Values* report](https://gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/publications/2022-01/incorporating_inuit_societal_values_report.pdf); [Parks Canada, Auyuittuq IQ page](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nu/auyuittuq/nature/conservation/autochtones-indigenous); [NIRB IQ page](https://www.nirb.ca/inuit-qaujimajatuqangit) **[verified via search summary]**
- The other GN principles (as commonly listed): **Inuuqatigiitsiarniq** (respecting others, relationships and caring for people); **Tunnganarniq** (fostering good spirits by being open, welcoming and inclusive); **Pilimmaksarniq/Pijariuqsarniq** (development of skills through practice, effort and action); **Qanuqtuurniq** (being innovative and resourceful). — [GN Guiding Principles page](https://prod.gov.nu.ca/en/mandate/guiding-principles) (unreachable this session) **[partly verified via search summary; Pilimmaksarniq and Qanuqtuurniq from prior knowledge]**
- NTI says non-Inuit researchers "should be better educated on Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit, ethical ways of working with Inuit". — [Arctic Focus](https://arcticfocus.org/stories/upholding-inuit-data-sovereignty-nunavut) **[verified]**

### Inferences
- A suggested mapping for slides (my synthesis, not from a source):
  - Piliriqatigiinniq ↔ CARE "Collective benefit" / ICC Protocol 6 (partnerships)
  - Aajiiqatigiinniq ↔ CARE "Authority to control" / FPIC (yes, yes-with-conditions, no)
  - Pijitsirniq ↔ CARE "Responsibility" (researchers serve community priorities)
  - Avatittinnik Kamatsiarniq ↔ stewardship of waters and the microbes in them
  - Pilimmaksarniq ↔ NISR Priority 5 (capacity), e.g. youth learning bioinformatics and naming
  - Qanuqtuurniq ↔ creative use of Inuktitut to coin names
- Have Inuit facilitators or Elders confirm these pairings rather than presenting them as settled.

### Gaps
- I could not reach gov.nu.ca to quote the official GN wording. Spelling variants exist (e.g. Piliriqatigiinniq vs Piliriqatigiingniq; Kivalliq dialect forms may differ).

## 6. UNDRIP articles (conceptual framing only)

### Takeaway
UNDRIP (UN General Assembly, 13 Sept 2007, A/RES/61/295) is the rights backbone cited by CARE, GIDA, ICC Protocol 1 and NISR. Article 31 is the key genetic-resources article. Article 13 is the key language article. Canada's *UN Declaration Act* (2021) commits Canada to implementing it. Legal force is covered by other researchers.

### Cited Findings
Article summaries (paraphrased from the UNDRIP text, https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples.html) **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]**:
- **Art. 11:** right to practise and revitalize cultural traditions and customs, including the right to maintain, protect and develop past, present and future manifestations of culture. States shall provide redress, including restitution, for cultural, intellectual, religious and spiritual property taken without FPIC.
- **Art. 13:** right to revitalize, use, develop and transmit to future generations their histories, **languages**, oral traditions, philosophies, writing systems and literatures, and to designate and retain their own names for communities, places and persons. This is the most relevant article for using Inuktitut in scientific names.
- **Art. 18:** right to participate in decision-making in matters affecting their rights, through representatives they choose, and to maintain their own decision-making institutions.
- **Art. 19:** States shall consult and cooperate in good faith to obtain free, prior and informed consent before adopting legislative or administrative measures that may affect them.
- **Art. 29:** right to the conservation and protection of the environment and the productive capacity of their lands, territories and resources.
- **Art. 31:** right to maintain, control, protect and develop their cultural heritage, traditional knowledge and traditional cultural expressions, "including human and **genetic resources**, seeds, medicines, knowledge of the properties of fauna and flora...", and to maintain, control, protect and develop their **intellectual property** over these.
- **Art. 32** (also relevant): right to determine priorities for development or use of their lands, territories and other resources.
- ICC Protocol 1 directs researchers to "Be familiar with and implement the provisions of the UNDRIP within all aspects of your work." — [EEE Protocols PDF p.17](https://lcipp.unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-06/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf) **[verified]**
- Canada's UN Declaration Act Action Plan (2023) commits to distinctions-based Indigenous data strategies. — [CSPS](https://www.csps-efpc.gc.ca/tools/articles/indigenous-data-sovereignty-eng.aspx) **[verified via search summary]**

### Inferences
- Art. 31's "genetic resources" is the usual conceptual hook for claiming Indigenous interests in non-human DNA, including microbial DNA. Art. 13 supports community authority over how Inuktitut words are used and spelled in names.

### Gaps
- Exact UNDRIP wording was not re-fetched this session. Copy the article text from the UN page before putting quotes on slides.

## 7. Biocultural rights, kaitiakitanga, and Indigenous genomics literature (non-human and microbial DNA)

### Takeaway
A substantial, mostly Māori-led and Native American-led literature argues that genetic resources (and the digital sequence data derived from them) from Indigenous lands and waters carry Indigenous rights and interests even when non-human. The key tools are guidelines (Te Nohonga Kaitiaki), metadata labels (Local Contexts TK/BC Labels and Notices), and controlled-access repositories (e.g. the Aotearoa Genomic Data Repository). Explicitly microbial cases are rarer, and I found no Inuit-specific microbial genomics framework.

### Cited Findings
- **Hudson et al. 2020.** Hudson M, Garrison NA, Sterling R, Caron NR, Fox K, Yracheta J, Anderson J, Wilcox P, Arbour L, Brown A, Taualii M, Kukutai T, Haring R, Te Aika B, Baynam GS, Dearden PK, Chagné D, Malhi RS, Garba I, Tiffin N, Bolnick D, Stott M, Rolleston AK, Ballantyne LL, Lovett R, David-Chavez D, Martinez A, Sporle A, Walter M, Reading J, Carroll SR. 2020. "Rights, interests and expectations: Indigenous perspectives on unrestricted access to genomic data." *Nature Reviews Genetics* 21:377–384. DOI: 10.1038/s41576-020-0228-x. It argues that unrestricted open-access genomic data (human and non-human) can conflict with Indigenous rights, and proposes CARE-aligned governance, benefit-sharing and tools such as Local Contexts labels. Co-author Matthew Stott is a microbiologist (geothermal microbes), and the paper discusses non-human and microbial resources. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified; the Stott/microbe detail is a recollection, so verify before using]**
- **Liggins, Hudson & Anderson 2021.** Liggins L, Hudson M, Anderson J. 2021. "Creating space for Indigenous perspectives on access and benefit-sharing: encouraging researcher use of the Local Contexts Notices." *Molecular Ecology* 30(11):2477–2482. DOI: 10.1111/mec.15918. Molecular ecologists can attach **Local Contexts Notices** (BC Notice, TK Notice) to samples and sequence data in repositories (e.g. GenBank BioProject/BioSample) to flag Indigenous provenance and invite future Indigenous governance, even before a community has applied its own Labels. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified]**
- **Carroll et al. 2021 *Sci Data*** (see section 1): machine-actionable CARE metadata. — DOI: 10.1038/s41597-021-00892-0 **[from prior knowledge]**

- **"Indigenous peoples and genomics: starting a conversation"** (Mc Cartney et al.). **[UNVERIFIED: I could not confirm the exact journal, year and author list this session. - I found no paper or statement combining microbiome or eDNA work with Inuit or Arctic Indigenous data sovereignty. The closest are the Frontiers in Genetics 2022 snippet on eDNA and the Māori/AGDR model. — search, Oct 2026 **[negative result]**

### Inferences
- Applying these to MAGs from Igluligaarjuk waters:
  1. Microbes are not "taonga" in an Inuit framework by default. Whether particular microbes or waters carry cultural significance is for the community to say, not researchers.
  2. The DNA comes from Inuit homelands and waters, so NISR P4 ("wildlife and environment"), ICC Protocol 7 ("resources and homelands", "raw ... scientific data") and UNDRIP Art. 31 ("genetic resources") all plausibly apply.
  3. A practical, low-cost step is attaching a **Local Contexts BC Notice** (and later community-applied BC Labels) to the NCBI/ENA BioProject and BioSamples, and to the SeqCode registry entries if supported.
  4. Any future commercial value from these sequences ties into the DSI and Cali Fund debate.
- The bush moa/AGDR example shows controlled access for non-human genomes is technically possible. The SeqCode, however, requires publicly available genome sequences for type material, so fully controlled access is incompatible with valid SeqCode naming. Raw reads or metadata could still carry Notices.

## 8. Language as data; critiques and tensions (open science vs sovereignty, helicopter research, shared waters)

### Takeaway
Words are data too. Indigenous language-data frameworks (notably Te Hiku Media's Kaitiakitanga Licence) and Inuit language rights (UNDRIP Art. 13; Nunavut's Inuit Language Protection Act) support community authority over how Inuktitut is used, spelled and attributed in permanent scientific names. The main tensions are: open-science deposition rules (INSDC, SeqCode) versus permission and review expectations (ICC P7); extractive "helicopter research"; and the question of whose data microbes in shared, flowing waters are.

### Cited Findings
**Language data**
- **Te Hiku Media Kaitiakitanga Licence** (Māori, Aotearoa). A licence framework for Māori language data (speech corpora) from a haukāinga (community) perspective. Data are used only for the benefit of Māori. Outside partners are bound by the licence, and use in applications that surveil, discriminate or violate human rights is barred. Te Hiku acts as kaitiaki (guardian) of the data. The licence text describes itself as a "living" work in progress, copyright Te Reo Irirangi o Te Hiku o Te Ika. — [ITU 2022](https://www.itu.int/hub/2022/08/ai-indigenous-languages-maori-te-reo/); [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/te-hiku-media-maori-speech-ai/); [GitHub mirror of licence/Rongo terms](https://worker-github.zxx930427.workers.dev/TeHikuMedia/Kaitiakitanga-License/blob/tumu/rongo.md) (official repo: github.com/TeHikuMedia/Kaitiakitanga-License) **[verified via search summaries; licence text not opened]**
- CARE R3 explicitly names "Indigenous languages and worldviews" as a responsibility. — Carroll et al. 2020 **[from prior knowledge]**
- ICC Protocol 4 calls for "plain language, translation and interpretation". Protocol 7 calls for reporting back "in appropriate and accessible language". The EEE document's copyright note treats IK concepts as collective IP. — [EEE PDF](https://lcipp.unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-06/EEE-Protocols-LR-WEB.pdf) **[verified]**
- Inuit language rights in Nunavut: the *Inuit Language Protection Act* (S.Nu. 2008, c.17) and the *Official Languages Act* (S.Nu. 2008, c.10). ITK's National Inuit Strategy on Research and its work on a unified Inuktut writing system (Inuktut Qaliujaaqpait, endorsed by ITK's Board in 2019) are relevant to romanization choices in names. — **[from prior knowledge, not re-verified; legal details covered by others]**
- UNDRIP Art. 13 covers languages, writing systems and the right "to designate and retain their own names". — see section 6 **[from prior knowledge]**

**Critiques and tensions**
- **Open science vs sovereignty.** Hudson et al. 2020 and Mc Cartney et al. 2022 argue that default unrestricted access ("open by default") can enable misappropriation and erode Indigenous authority. They propose "as open as possible, as closed as necessary", with provenance and permission metadata, rather than blanket closure. Kukutai & Black 2024 shows a controlled-access alternative (AGDR). — DOIs above **[Hudson/Mc Cartney from prior knowledge; Kukutai & Black verified]**
- **Helicopter or parachute research.** ICC developed the EEE Protocols in response to long-running frustration with outside researchers and visitors who engaged Inuit communities too little. — [Arctic Today](https://www.arctictoday.com/?p=52850); [ICC press release](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/news/inuit-circumpolar-council-establishes-new-standard-in-international-engagement-with-the-release-of-circumpolar-inuit-protocols-for-equitable-and-ethical-engagement/) **[verified]**. Protocol 5 criticizes leaving "without sharing the data that has been collected". — EEE PDF p.24 **[verified, partial line]**
- **IK vs science.** ICC Protocol 2 says IK "must not be translated, integrated into, or validated by science". This is a caution against framing Inuktitut names or knowledge as raw material for science. — EEE PDF p.18 **[verified]**
- **Indigenous Peoples vs "local communities".** ICC opposes conflating the two in the CBD (relevant to who receives DSI benefits). — [ICC Canada 2025–26 Annual Report](https://www.inuitcircumpolar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026-AnnualReport-Digital-ENG-Final.pdf) **[verified]**

### Inferences
- **Whose data is microbial DNA from shared waters?** No source found resolves this. Arguments in favour of Inuit governance are NISR's "wildlife and environment" scope, ICC P7's "resources and homelands" and "data collected within Inuit Nunaat", and Nunavut Agreement rights to the Nunavut Settlement Area marine areas (legal detail covered by others). Complicating factors: marine microbes disperse across jurisdictions; the same or similar taxa likely occur elsewhere; and MAGs are reconstructions produced with researchers' labour and tools. A defensible workshop position is "shared stewardship with Inuit authority over local samples, metadata, names and benefits", rather than claiming exclusive ownership of a species.
- **Names as language data.** A SeqCode name, once validly published, is permanent and global and cannot be retracted. That makes FPIC and community review of the word, its meaning, spelling, romanization and attribution (e.g. etymology crediting the community or speakers) especially important. Recording each name's etymology, the speaker or Elder consulted, and community approval is a concrete CARE and ICC P7 practice.
- Helicopter-research risk can be mitigated by the workshop format itself (community naming, report-back, youth capacity), if it follows through on ICC P5 and P7: returning data, local copies, and plain-language and Inuktitut summaries.

### Gaps
- I found no framework specifically on Indigenous-language words in **biological nomenclature** (a SeqCode or ICNP policy on Indigenous-language etymology and consent). Other researchers may cover nomenclature codes.
- Te Hiku licence text was not read directly.
- I found no Inuit organization statement specifically on use of Inuktitut in scientific names.
